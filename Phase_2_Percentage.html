📅 Days 13–20 (8 Days) — Day-by-Day Conceptual Master Plan

Day 13
Fraction ↔ % Mastery & Multiplier Method
📝 25 Mental Drills
Standard Fraction Equivalents & Multiplying Factors
  • The Core Foundation: Percentage means "per hundred" (प्रति सौ). A fraction $\frac{a}{b}$ converted to percentage $= \frac{a}{b} \times 100\%$.
  • Memorized Equivalents: $1/2=50\%$, $1/3=33.33\%$, $1/4=25\%$, $1/5=20\%$, $1/6=16.67\%$, $1/7=14.28\%$, $1/8=12.5\%$, $1/9=11.11\%$, $1/10=10\%$, $1/11=9.09\%$, $1/12=8.33\%$, $1/13=7.69\%$, $1/14=7.14\%$, $1/15=6.67\%$, $1/16=6.25\%$, $1/20=5\%$, $1/25=4\%$.
  • Derived Fractions: $3/8 = 37.5\%$, $5/8 = 62.5\%$, $7/8 = 87.5\%$, $2/3 = 66.67\%$, $4/7 = 57.14\%$, $5/6 = 83.33\%$.
  • Multiplying Factor (MF): • $+20\% \implies MF = 1 + 0.20 = 1.20 = \frac{6}{5}$. (New value $= \text{Old} \times \frac{6}{5}$).
    • $-16.67\% \implies MF = 1 - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$. (New value $= \text{Old} \times \frac{5}{6}$).
  • Symmetric Commutative Law: $X\% \text{ of } Y = Y\% \text{ of } X$. (e.g. $64\% \text{ of } 25 = 25\% \text{ of } 64 = \frac{64}{4} = 16$! Mentally solved!).
🎯 Day 13 Focus: Eliminate pencil calculation for standard percentages ($12.5\%, 16.67\%, 37.5\%$) by converting to fractions instantly.
Day 14
% Increase/Decrease & Base Change Duality
📝 20 Invariance Qs
Base Inversion & The Golden $+1/x \leftrightarrow -1/(x+1)$ Rule
  • The Base Comparison Rule: "A is $x\%$ more than B" $\implies$ Base is B. "B is what $\%$ less than A?" $\implies$ Base shifts to A.
  • The Shift Duality Law: If $A$ is greater than $B$ by fraction $+\frac{a}{b}$, then $B$ is smaller than $A$ by fraction $-\frac{a}{b+a}$.
    • If $A$ is $+25\%$ ($+\frac{1}{4}$) more than $B$, then $B$ is $-\frac{1}{4+1} = -\frac{1}{5} = -20\%$ less than $A$.
    • If $A$ is $+33.33\%$ ($+\frac{1}{3}$) more than $B$, then $B$ is $-\frac{1}{3+1} = -\frac{1}{4} = -25\%$ less than $A$.
    • If $A$ is $-20\%$ ($-\frac{1}{5}$) less than $B$, then $B$ is $+\frac{1}{5-1} = +\frac{1}{4} = +25\%$ more than $A$.
  • Reverse Percentage: If a quantity increases by $15\%$ to become $1150$, original value $= \frac{1150}{1.15} = 1000$. Never subtract $15\%$ from $1150$!
🎯 Day 14 Focus: Instant answer in 5 seconds without writing any equation for all comparative questions.
Day 15
Successive Percentage Changes & 2D/3D Scaling
📝 25 Formula Drills
The $(a + b + \frac{ab}{100})$ Engine & Ratio Chain Method
  • Two Successive Changes: $\text{Net }\% = a + b + \frac{ab}{100}\%$. (Use $+$ for increase, $-$ for decrease).
    • Two increases $+20\%$ and $+10\%$: $20 + 10 + \frac{200}{100} = +32\%$.
    • One increase $+20\%$, one decrease $-10\%$: $20 - 10 - \frac{200}{100} = +8\%$.
    • An increase $+x\%$ followed by decrease $-x\%$: ALWAYS a net decrease of $-\frac{x^2}{100}\%$.
  • Three Successive Changes ($a, b, c$): • Method 1: Combine first two $(a, b) \to R$, then combine $(R, c)$.
    • Method 2 (Ratio Method - Recommended for fractions): If values are fractional like $12.5\%, 16.67\%$, multiply initial to final ratios: $\text{Initial} \times \frac{9}{8} \times \frac{7}{6} \times \dots$
  • 2D Geometry Area Scaling: • Rectangle Area $= L \times B \implies$ Net change in area $= l + b + \frac{lb}{100}$.
    • Circle Area $= \pi r^2 \implies$ If radius increases by $r\%$, Area increases by $2r + \frac{r^2}{100}\%$.
🎯 Day 15 Focus: Choosing between the formula method and ratio method based on the percentages given.
Day 16
Product Constancy (A × B = Constant)
📝 20 Consumption Qs
Price × Consumption = Constant Expenditure
  • The Product Constancy Principle: Whenever $A \times B = C$ and $C$ remains CONSTANT, $A$ and $B$ are inversely proportional ($A \propto \frac{1}{B}$).
  • Direct Applications in SSC: • $\text{Price} \times \text{Consumption} = \text{Expenditure}$ (when expenditure constant).
    • $\text{Speed} \times \text{Time} = \text{Distance}$ (when distance constant).
    • $\text{Rate} \times \text{Time} = \text{Total Work}$ (when work constant).
    • $\text{Length} \times \text{Breadth} = \text{Area}$ (when area constant).
  • Direct Shortcut: If price increases by $x\% = +\frac{a}{b}$, consumption MUST decrease by $\frac{a}{b+a} \times 100\%$ to maintain constant budget.
    Example: Price of sugar rises by $25\%$ ($+1/4$). To keep expenditure same, consumption must decrease by $\frac{1}{4+1} = \frac{1}{5} = 20\%$!
🎯 Day 16 Focus: "Sugar price rises, family buys 4 kg less for Rs. 800 — find original and reduced price per kg."
Day 17
Price–Consumption with Variable Budget
📝 20 Advanced Qs
When Expenditure Also Changes (The 100-Baseline Line)
  • The Problem Type: Price of an item increases by $P\%$, but the family allows expenditure to increase by only $E\%$. Find $\%$ change in consumption.
  • The 100-Baseline Line Shortcut: $$\text{Old Price Base } (100) \xrightarrow{+\text{Price } P} (100+P) \xrightarrow{\text{Consumption Change}} (100+E) \xleftarrow{+\text{Expenditure } E} (100)$$ $$\mathbf{\% \text{ Change in Consumption}} = \frac{(100 + E) - (100 + P)}{100 + P} \times 100\%$$ Example: Sugar price increases by $30\%$, but expenditure increases by only $10\%$.
    Consumption change $= \frac{110 - 130}{130} = -\frac{20}{130} = -\frac{2}{13} = -15.38\%$. (No $x,y$ algebra needed!).
  • Finding Quantities (Kg): If family buys $X$ kg less, $\frac{2}{13}$ of original consumption $= X$ kg $\implies$ Original consumption $= \frac{13}{2} X$ kg.
🎯 Day 17 Focus: Solving Tier-2 multi-variable consumption questions in under 30 seconds.
Day 18
Income, Expenditure, Savings & Income Tax
📝 20 Applied Drills
Income Pipeline & The Income Tax Cross-Ratio Invariance
  • The Income Balance Equation: $\text{Income} = \text{Expenditure} + \text{Savings}$.
    • If Income increases by $I\%$, Expenditure by $E\%$, find $\%$ change in Savings.
    • ⚡ Alligation / Weighted Shortcut: $\frac{\text{Exp}}{\text{Sav}} = \frac{\Delta \text{Sav} - \Delta \text{Inc}}{\Delta \text{Inc} - \Delta \text{Exp}}$.
  • Income Tax & Net Income Rule: $\text{Gross Income} = \text{Tax} + \text{Net Income}$.
    Since Gross Income is constant, $\mathbf{\Delta \text{Tax Amount} = \Delta \text{Net Income Drop}}$.
    $$\text{Tax} \times T\% = \text{Net Income} \times N\% \implies \frac{\text{Tax}}{\text{Net Income}} = \frac{N}{T}$$ $$\mathbf{\text{Rate of Income Tax}} = \frac{\text{Tax}}{\text{Tax} + \text{Net Income}} \times 100\% = \frac{N}{N + T} \times 100\%$$ Example: Tax rate increases by $19\%$, Net income decreases by $1\%$. Rate of tax $= \frac{1}{1 + 19} \times 100 = \frac{1}{20} \times 100 = 5\%$! Instant 2-second answer!
  • Population Compounding: $P_n = P_0 \left(1 \pm \frac{r}{100}\right)^n$. Treated identically to Compound Interest.
🎯 Day 18 Focus: Income tax questions and 3-step expenditure-savings shift problems.
Day 19
Election Problems & Venn Diagram Models
📝 20 Exam Models
Voter Roll Pipeline, Margin Equations & Set Intersections
  • The Election Waterfall Hierarchy: $$\text{Total Voters on Voter List (100\%)} \xrightarrow{-\text{Did Not Vote}} \text{Polled Votes} \xrightarrow{-\text{Invalid Votes}} \mathbf{\text{Valid Votes}}$$
  • Victory Margin: Winner's votes $-$ Runner-up's votes $=$ Victory Margin. Unless specified, Winner's percentage is calculated on VALID VOTES!
  • Venn Diagram Percentages: • For 2 sets $A$ and $B$: $n(A \cup B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A \cap B)$.
    • Passed in English ($E\%$), Passed in Maths ($M\%$), Passed in Both ($B\%$): Failed in both $= 100 - [E + M - B]$.

📐 Formula Vault (7 Master Formulations)

📐 Master Fraction-Percentage Equivalents Table
1/3=33.33% | 1/6=16.67% | 1/7=14.28% | 1/8=12.5% | 1/9=11.11% | 1/11=9.09% | 1/16=6.25%
Multipliers: $+x\% \to (1 + x/100)$  |  $-x\% \to (1 - x/100)$.
⚡ Key Derivations: $3/8 = 37.5\%$, $5/8 = 62.5\%$, $7/8 = 87.5\%$, $2/3 = 66.67\%$, $5/6 = 83.33\%$.
🚨 Trap: Confusing $1/9 = 11.11\%$ with $1/11 = 9.09\%$. Remember: 9 in denominator has 11s, 11 in denominator has 9s.
📐 Base Reversal & Change Duality (+1/n ↔ -1/(n+1))
Increase of +a/b ↔ Required Decrease of -a/(b+a) | Increase of +1/n ↔ Decrease of -1/(n+1)
When to use: Whenever product of two variables is constant, or when comparing two numbers on different bases.
⚡ Application: Salary of A is $25\%$ ($+1/4$) more than B $\implies$ Salary of B is $\frac{1}{4+1} = \frac{1}{5} = 20\%$ less than A. Instant!
🚨 Trap: Students incorrectly calculate $25\%$ on A's salary! Base is shifted to A, so denominator increases.
📐 Successive Percentage Change Engine
Net% = a + b + (ab/100)  |  Equal Increase & Decrease: Net = -(x²/100)%
Sign Convention: $+$ for increase, $-$ for decrease.
⚡ 3 Successive Changes: Combine $(a, b) \to R$, then combine $(R, c)$. Or use Ratio Chain: $\frac{\text{Final}}{\text{Initial}} = MF_1 \times MF_2 \times MF_3$.
🚨 Trap: Direct addition like $+20\%$ and $-20\% = 0\%$ is WRONG. Actual net is $-20^2/100 = -4\%$.
📐 Price–Consumption–Expenditure Master Equation
ΔConsumption% = [(100 + E) - (100 + P)] / (100 + P) × 100%  [P=%Price, E=%Expenditure]
When to use: When price increases by $P\%$ and total budget is allowed to increase by only $E\%$.
⚡ Constant Budget ($E=0$): Consumption change $= \frac{-P}{100+P} \times 100\%$.
🚨 Trap: Treating consumption decrease as $(P - E)\%$ directly. Must divide by the new price base $(100 + P)$!
📐 Income Tax & Net Income Ratio Invariance
Initial Tax Rate% = [N / (N + T)] × 100%  [T = Tax rise%, N = Net income drop%]
Derivation: $\Delta \text{Tax Amount} = \Delta \text{Net Income Drop} \implies \text{Tax} \times T = \text{Net} \times N \implies \frac{\text{Tax}}{\text{Net}} = \frac{N}{T}$.
⚡ Example: Tax increases by $19\%$, Net income drops by $1\% \implies \frac{1}{1 + 19} = \frac{1}{20} = 5\%$.
🚨 Trap: Inverting the ratio ($T/(N+T)$) results in 95% instead of 5%. Always put net income reduction on top.
📐 Election Master Hierarchy & Margin Formula
Victory Margin = (Winner% - Runner-Up%) × Valid Votes
When to use: Elections with two candidates. Note: $(\text{Winner\%} + \text{Runner-Up\%}) = 100\%$ of valid votes.
⚡ Margin Shortcut: If Winner gets $56\%$ of valid votes, Runner-Up gets $44\%$. Margin $= 56 - 44 = 12\%$ of valid votes!
🚨 Trap: Applying winner's percentage to total voters on list when problem states "winner got 54% of VALID votes".
📐 Venn Diagram 2-Set & 3-Set Formulas
n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A ∩ B)  |  Failed in Both% = 100% - n(Passed in A ∪ B)
When to use: Exam pass/fail, newspaper readership, language proficiency percentage questions.
⚡ Shortcut: Convert all given data to ONE uniform state (either all "Passed" or all "Failed") before drawing Venn diagram.
🚨 Trap: Mixing "Passed in English" with "Failed in Maths" in the same Venn equation.

⚡ Speed Shortcuts & Exam Hacks

⚡ 1. The X% of Y = Y% of X Symmetry Hack
Whenever calculating an awkward percentage of a round number, swap them!
• $84\% \text{ of } 50 = 50\% \text{ of } 84 = 42$.
• $18\% \text{ of } 150 = 150\% \text{ of } 18 = 18 + 9 = 27$.
• $48\% \text{ of } 25 = 25\% \text{ of } 48 = 12$. Saves huge calculation time!
⚡ 2. The Golden +1/n Shift for Product Constancy
Price rises $\implies$ consumption falls:
$+1/2 (50\%) \to -1/3 (33.33\%)$
$+1/3 (33.33\%) \to -1/4 (25\%)$
$+1/4 (25\%) \to -1/5 (20\%)$
$+1/5 (20\%) \to -1/6 (16.67\%)$
$+1/6 (16.67\%) \to -1/7 (14.28\%)$. Memorize this ladder!
⚡ 3. The Sugar/Price Quantity (Kg) Shortcut
"A reduction of $20\%$ in price enables a buyer to obtain 4 kg more for Rs. 160":
• Reduced Price per kg $= \frac{\text{Money} \times \%\text{Reduction}}{\text{Quantity}} = \frac{160 \times 20\%}{4} = \frac{32}{4} = \text{Rs. } 8/\text{kg}$!
• Original Price $= \frac{\text{Reduced Price}}{1 - 0.20} = \frac{8}{0.8} = \text{Rs. } 10/\text{kg}$!
⚡ 4. Income = Expenditure + Savings Alligation Cross
Expenditure increases by $E\%$, Savings by $S\%$, Income by $I\%$.
Set up alligation cross: $|S - I|$ and $|I - E|$.
$$\frac{\text{Expenditure}}{\text{Savings}} = \frac{S - I}{I - E}$$ Directly gives the original ratio of expenditure to savings without any algebra!
⚡ 5. Election Margin Valid Votes Calculation
In an election with 2 candidates, winner got $W\%$ of valid votes. Then loser got $(100 - W)\%$.
$$\text{Margin} = (2W - 100)\% \times \text{Valid Votes}$$ Example: Winner got $58\% \implies$ Margin $= (2 \times 58 - 100)\% = 16\%$ of valid votes.
⚡ 6. Successive 2D/3D Volume Scaling Chain
For $V = L \times B \times H$, use fractional multipliers:
If $L +20\% (\times 6/5)$, $B -10\% (\times 9/10)$, $H +15\% (\times 23/20)$:
$$\frac{V_{\text{new}}}{V_{\text{old}}} = \frac{6}{5} \times \frac{9}{10} \times \frac{23}{20} = \frac{1242}{1000} = 1.242 \implies +24.2\%!$$

🚨 SSC Exam Trap Library (6 Verified Traps)

🚨 Trap 1: Base Reversal Re-subtraction Error-C
"A's salary is $20\%$ more than B. By what $\%$ is B's salary less than A?"
Students answer: $20\%$. FATAL ERROR. Correct answer: $\frac{20}{120} \times 100 = 16.67\%$. Base is A ($120$), not B ($100$).
🚨 Trap 2: Direct Addition of Successive Percentages Error-F
A salary increased by $10\%$ and then increased by $10\%$. Students say $20\%$. Actual: $10 + 10 + \frac{100}{100} = 21\%$. Never add percentages directly!
🚨 Trap 3: Election Majority on Total vs Valid Votes Error-R
Carefully read: "Winner secured $54\%$ of total votes polled" vs "Winner secured $54\%$ of VALID votes". If $10\%$ votes were invalid, applying $54\%$ to polled votes ruins the entire equation.
🚨 Trap 4: Variable Expenditure Shortcut Misuse Error-K
When price rises $20\%$ and expenditure rises $8\%$, students do: $20 - 8 = 12\%$ consumption decrease. WRONG. Correct is: $\frac{108 - 120}{120} = -\frac{12}{120} = -10\%$.
🚨 Trap 5: Income Tax Ratio Inversion Error-C
Tax increases by $19\%$, net income drops by $1\%$. Rate of tax $= \frac{1}{1+19} = 5\%$. Students calculate $\frac{19}{20} = 95\%$, forgetting that tax rate is $\frac{\text{Tax}}{\text{Gross}}$.
🚨 Trap 6: Multi-Stage Reduction in Population Error-T
Population decreases by $10\%$ first year and $10\%$ second year. It does NOT decrease by $20\%$. It decreases by $10 + 10 - \frac{100}{100} = 19\%$.

(iii) $37.5\% = \frac{3}{8} \implies \frac{3}{8} \times 720 = 3 \times 90 = 270$.

Q2: If A's salary is $25\%$ more than B's salary, then B's salary is how much percent less than A's salary?
Hint: Apply $+1/n \to -1/(n+1)$ shift rule: $+25\% = +1/4$.

• Method 1 (Duality Shift): $+25\% = +\frac{1}{4} \implies -\frac{1}{4+1} = -\frac{1}{5} = -20\%$.
• Method 2 (Base Method): Let $B = 100 \implies A = 125$.
Difference $= 125 - 100 = 25$.
$\text{Percentage less than A} = \frac{25}{125} \times 100 = \frac{1}{5} \times 100 = 20\%$.

Q3: The price of an article was first increased by $20\%$ and later decreased by $20\%$. If the final price is Rs. 960, find the original price.
Hint: Equal increase and decrease by x% gives net change = -(x²/100)%.
Answer: Rs. 1000

2. Final price represents $(100 - 4)\% = 96\%$ of original price.
3. $96\% = 960 \implies 1\% = 10 \implies 100\% = 1000$.
Original price $= \text{Rs. } 1000$.

Answer: 20%

• Price change $= +25\% = +\frac{1}{4}$.
• Consumption change $= -\frac{1}{4+1} = -\frac{1}{5} = -20\%$.
The family must reduce consumption by $20\%$.

Q5: The price of petrol increases by $28\%$. A motorist wants to increase his expenditure by only $12\%$. By what percentage (rounded to 1 decimal) should he decrease his consumption?
Hint: Use the 100-baseline: ΔConsumption% = [(100 + E) - (100 + P)] / (100 + P) × 100%.
Answer: 12.5% decrease
• Price index $= 100 + 28 = 128$.
• Target expenditure index $= 100 + 12 = 112$.
• Consumption must change from $128$ to $112$:
$$\Delta C = \frac{112 - 128}{128} = -\frac{16}{128} = -\frac{1}{8} = -12.5\%$$ He must reduce consumption by $12.5\%$.

Level B: Standard Tier-1
Q6: A reduction of $20\%$ in the price of apples enables a person to buy 5 kg more apples for Rs. 500. Find: (i) the reduced price per kg, (ii) the original price per kg.
Hint: Reduced price per kg = (Total Money × % Reduction) / Quantity.
Answer: Reduced Price = Rs. 20/kg, Original Price = Rs. 25/kg
1. Reduced Price:
Money saved $= 20\% \text{ of } 500 = \text{Rs. } 100$.
This Rs. 100 buys 5 extra kg $\implies \text{Reduced Price} = \frac{100}{5} = \mathbf{Rs.\ 20/kg}$.
2. Original Price:
Since price was reduced by $20\%$, Rs. 20 is $80\%$ of original price:
$$\text{Original Price} = \frac{20}{0.80} = \mathbf{Rs.\ 25/kg}$$.

Level C: SSC Exam Level
Q7: A man spends $75\%$ of his income. His income increases by $20\%$ and his expenditure increases by $10\%$. Find the percentage increase in his savings.
Hint: Assume initial Income = 100, Expenditure = 75, Savings = 25. Calculate new values.
Answer: 50%
• Initial: Income $= 100$, Expenditure $= 75$, Savings $= 25$.
• New Income: $100 + 20\% = 120$.
• New Expenditure: $75 + (10\% \text{ of } 75) = 75 + 7.5 = 82.5$.
• New Savings: $120 - 82.5 = 37.5$.
• Increase in Savings: $37.5 - 25 = 12.5$.
$$\% \text{ Increase in Savings} = \frac{12.5}{25} \times 100\% = 50\%!$$
