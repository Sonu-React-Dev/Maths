📅 Days 32–39 (8 Days) — Day-by-Day Conceptual Master Plan

Day 32
CP, SP, MP & Percentage Bases
📝 20 Foundation Drills
Cost Price, Selling Price, Marked Price & The Multiplier Method
  • The Three Core Prices: • Cost Price (CP / क्रय मूल्य): The total acquisition expenditure (purchase price $+$ transport/repairs). CP is ALWAYS the baseline ($100\%$) for standard profit and loss calculations.
    • Marked Price (MP / अंकित मूल्य / List Price): The printed sticker tag price. MP is ALWAYS the baseline for discount calculations.
    • Selling Price (SP / विक्रय मूल्य): The actual transaction price at which the good is exchanged.
  • Fundamental Equations: • $\text{Profit} = \text{SP} - \text{CP}$ (when $\text{SP} > \text{CP}$).
    • $\text{Loss} = \text{CP} - \text{SP}$ (when $\text{CP} > \text{SP}$).
    • $\text{Discount} = \text{MP} - \text{SP}$ (when $\text{MP} \ge \text{SP}$).
  • Multiplying Factor (MF) for Profit/Loss: • Profit of $25\% \implies \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times 1.25 = \text{CP} \times \frac{5}{4}$.
    • Loss of $16\frac{2}{3}\% \implies \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \left(1 - \frac{1}{6}\right) = \text{CP} \times \frac{5}{6}$.
    • Discount of $20\% \implies \text{SP} = \text{MP} \times 0.80 = \text{MP} \times \frac{4}{5}$.
  • Worked Micro-Example: A man buys an old cycle for ₹1200, spends ₹300 on repairs, and sells it for ₹1800. What is his profit percentage?
    Effective $\text{CP} = 1200 + 300 = \text{₹}1500$. $\text{SP} = 1800$.
    $\text{Profit} = 1800 - 1500 = 300 \implies \text{Profit } \% = \frac{300}{1500} \times 100 = 20\%$. (Never ignore repair cost in CP!).
🎯 Day 32 Focus: Remember that overhead expenses (repairs, freight, taxes) are always added into the Cost Price before computing profit or loss.
Day 33
Profit % & "CP of X = SP of Y"
📝 25 Ratio Drills
Percentage Formulations & Direct Equated Ratio Inversion
  • Standard Percentage Formulas: $$\mathbf{\text{Profit } \% = \frac{\text{SP} - \text{CP}}{\text{CP}} \times 100\%} \quad \Big| \quad \mathbf{\text{Loss } \% = \frac{\text{CP} - \text{SP}}{\text{CP}} \times 100\%}$$
  • The Classical "CP of $x$ = SP of $y$" Model: If the cost price of $x$ articles equals the selling price of $y$ articles:
    $$x \cdot \text{CP} = y \cdot \text{SP} \implies \mathbf{\frac{\text{SP}}{\text{CP}} = \frac{x}{y}}$$ • If $x > y \implies$ Profit of $\frac{x - y}{y} \times 100\%$.
    • If $x < y \implies$ Loss of $\frac{y - x}{y} \times 100\%$.
  • Worked Micro-Example: "Cost price of 15 tables is equal to selling price of 12 tables. Find profit %."
    $15 \cdot \text{CP} = 12 \cdot \text{SP} \implies \frac{\text{SP}}{\text{CP}} = \frac{15}{12} = \frac{5}{4}$.
    $\text{CP} = 4\text{ units}, \text{SP} = 5\text{ units} \implies \text{Profit} = 1\text{ unit}$.
    $\text{Profit } \% = \frac{1}{4} \times 100 = 25\%$. Solved in 5 seconds without assuming ₹100 or ₹1!
  • Profit Calculated on SP (Exam Trap Model): If a vendor calculates his profit as $20\%$ on Selling Price, what is his actual profit % on Cost Price?
    Let $\text{SP} = 100$. Profit $= 20\% \text{ of } 100 = 20 \implies \text{CP} = 100 - 20 = 80$.
    Actual $\text{Profit } \% \text{ on CP} = \frac{20}{80} \times 100 = 25\%$!
🎯 Day 33 Focus: Watch out for questions stating "profit on selling price" — convert immediately to base CP using $\text{CP} = \text{SP} - \text{Profit}$.
Day 34
Markup %, Discount % & CP-MP-SP Chain
📝 25 Chain Drills
Markup Mechanics, Discount Formulas & The Complete Transaction Line
  • Markup Percentage (अंकित मूल्य वृद्धि %): $$\mathbf{\text{Markup } \% = \frac{\text{MP} - \text{CP}}{\text{CP}} \times 100\%} \implies \text{MP} = \text{CP} \times \left(1 + \frac{M}{100}\right)$$
  • Discount Percentage (छूट / बट्टा %): $$\mathbf{\text{Discount } \% = \frac{\text{MP} - \text{SP}}{\text{MP}} \times 100\%} \implies \text{SP} = \text{MP} \times \left(1 - \frac{D}{100}\right)$$
  • The Complete Transaction Chain: $$\text{CP} \xrightarrow{\text{+ Markup } M\%} \text{MP} \xrightarrow{\text{- Discount } D\%} \text{SP}$$ Combined equation: $\mathbf{\text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \left(1 + \frac{M}{100}\right) \times \left(1 - \frac{D}{100}\right)}$.
  • Net Profit % from Markup & Discount: Using successive formula: $\mathbf{\text{Net } \% = M - D - \frac{MD}{100}\%}$.
    If positive $\implies$ Profit %; if negative $\implies$ Loss %.
  • Worked Micro-Example: A shopkeeper marks goods $30\%$ above CP and allows a $10\%$ discount. What is his net profit %?
    $\text{Net } \% = 30 - 10 - \frac{30 \times 10}{100} = 20 - 3 = 17\%$ profit. Solved mentally in 4 seconds!
🎯 Day 34 Focus: Never calculate discount on CP! Discount is strictly deducted from Marked Price (MP).
Day 35
Successive Discounts & "Buy X Get Y Free"
📝 25 Discount Drills
Single Equivalent Discount, Successive Chains & Free Promotional Schemes
  • Two Successive Discounts ($d_1\%$ and $d_2\%$): $$\mathbf{D_{\text{eq}} = d_1 + d_2 - \frac{d_1 d_2}{100}\%}$$ Example: Successive discounts of $20\%$ and $10\% = 20 + 10 - \frac{200}{100} = 28\%$ (NOT $30\%$).
  • Three Successive Discounts ($d_1, d_2, d_3$): Use the multiplier fraction method: $\text{SP} = \text{MP} \times \left(\frac{100 - d_1}{100}\right) \times \left(\frac{100 - d_2}{100}\right) \times \left(\frac{100 - d_3}{100}\right)$.
    Example: $20\%, 15\%, 10\% \implies \text{SP} = \text{MP} \times \frac{4}{5} \times \frac{17}{20} \times \frac{9}{10} = \text{MP} \times \frac{612}{1000} \implies D_{\text{eq}} = 100 - 61.2 = 38.8\%$.
  • The "Buy $X$ Get $Y$ Free" Formula: $$\mathbf{\text{Effective Discount } \% = \frac{\text{Free Articles}}{\text{Total Articles Received}} \times 100\% = \frac{Y}{X + Y} \times 100\%}$$ • "Buy 3 Get 1 Free": $\frac{1}{3 + 1} \times 100 = \frac{1}{4} \times 100 = 25\%$.
    • "Buy 5 Get 3 Free": $\frac{3}{5 + 3} \times 100 = \frac{3}{8} \times 100 = 37.5\%$.
  • Combined Free Articles + Extra % Discount: "Buy 4 Get 1 Free and an additional $20\%$ discount":
    $D_1 = \frac{1}{5} \times 100 = 20\%$. $D_2 = 20\%$.
    $D_{\text{net}} = 20 + 20 - \frac{20 \times 20}{100} = 40 - 4 = 36\%$.
🎯 Day 35 Focus: In "Buy $X$ Get $Y$ Free", the denominator is ALWAYS the total count $(X+Y)$, not just the paid count $X$.
Day 36
The Golden CP / MP Ratio Formula
📝 25 Formula Drills
The Universal Direct Equation: Connecting Cost Price to Marked Price
  • Mathematical Derivation: $\text{SP} = \text{MP} \times \left(\frac{100 - D}{100}\right)$ and $\text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \left(\frac{100 + P}{100}\right)$.
    Equating both expressions for $\text{SP}$ gives: $$\mathbf{\frac{\text{CP}}{\text{MP}} = \frac{100 - D\%}{100 + P\%}}$$
  • Variant with Loss ($L\%$): $$\mathbf{\frac{\text{CP}}{\text{MP}} = \frac{100 - D\%}{100 - L\%}}$$
  • Why This is SSC's Most Powerful Shortcut: Eliminates calculating $\text{SP}$ entirely! Directly links $\text{CP}$, $\text{MP}$, Discount $\%$, and Profit $\%$.
  • Worked Micro-Example: A shopkeeper allows a $10\%$ discount on MP and still makes a profit of $20\%$. By what percentage is the MP marked above the CP?
    $\frac{\text{CP}}{\text{MP}} = \frac{100 - 10}{100 + 20} = \frac{90}{120} = \frac{3}{4}$.
    $\text{CP} = 3\text{ units}, \text{MP} = 4\text{ units}$.
    Markup $= \frac{4 - 3}{3} \times 100 = \frac{1}{3} \times 100 = 33.33\%$. Solved in 6 seconds!
  • Finding Price Directly: If $\text{CP} = \text{₹}450$, find $\text{MP}$: $\text{MP} = 450 \times \frac{4}{3} = \text{₹}600$.
🎯 Day 36 Focus: Whenever both Discount % and Profit % appear together in a question, write $\frac{\text{CP}}{\text{MP}} = \frac{100-D}{100+P}$ immediately.
Day 37
Dishonest Dealer & False Weight Fraud
📝 20 Fraud Analysis Qs
Single-Weight Cheating, Dual Pricing Fraud & Multiplier Multiplexing
  • Pure False Weight Profit Formula: When selling at nominal cost price but using false weight: $$\mathbf{\text{Profit } \% = \frac{\text{True Weight} - \text{False Weight}}{\text{False Weight}} \times 100\% = \frac{\text{Error}}{\text{False Value}} \times 100\%}$$ Example: A trader claims to sell at CP but uses a $900\text{ g}$ weight instead of $1\text{ kg} (1000\text{ g})$:
    $\text{Profit } \% = \frac{1000 - 900}{900} \times 100 = \frac{100}{900} \times 100 = \frac{1}{9} \times 100 = 11.11\%$.
  • The Multiplier Ratio Method (Universal for Complex Fraud): Represent every action as a ratio of $\frac{\text{SP}}{\text{CP}}$:
    • Price profit $+x\% \implies \frac{\text{SP}}{\text{CP}} = \frac{100 + x}{100}$.
    • False weight fraud $\implies \frac{\text{SP}}{\text{CP}} = \frac{\text{True Weight}}{\text{Given False Weight}}$.
    • Overall Ratio: $\left(\frac{\text{SP}}{\text{CP}}\right)_{\text{net}} = \left(\frac{\text{SP}}{\text{CP}}\right)_{\text{price}} \times \left(\frac{\text{SP}}{\text{CP}}\right)_{\text{weight}}$.
  • Worked Micro-Example (Double Fraud): A merchant marks goods $20\%$ above CP and also gives only $800\text{ g}$ instead of $1000\text{ g}$. What is his total profit %?
    $\left(\frac{\text{SP}}{\text{CP}}\right)_{\text{price}} = \frac{120}{100} = \frac{6}{5}$.
    $\left(\frac{\text{SP}}{\text{CP}}\right)_{\text{weight}} = \frac{1000}{800} = \frac{5}{4}$.
    $\left(\frac{\text{SP}}{\text{CP}}\right)_{\text{net}} = \frac{6}{5} \times \frac{5}{4} = \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2}$.
    $\text{Profit } \% = \frac{3 - 2}{2} \times 100 = 50\%$ net profit! Clean and foolproof.
🎯 Day 37 Focus: In weight fraud, the cost to the shopkeeper is the FALSE weight he actually gives away, which is why false weight is in the denominator.
Day 38
Equal SP Models & Split Quantity Cases
📝 25 Advanced Qs
Two Articles Sold at Equal SP ($+x\%$ and $-x\%$), Weighted Splits & Break-Even
  • The Equal SP Symmetry Theorem: When two articles are sold at the SAME Selling Price (SP), one at a profit of $x\%$ and the other at a loss of $x\%$:
    $$\mathbf{\text{Overall Result is ALWAYS a Net Loss of } -\frac{x^2}{100}\%}$$ Example: Two cars sold for ₹2,40,000 each: one at $20\%$ profit, one at $20\%$ loss $\implies$ Net loss $= \frac{20^2}{100} = 4\%$ loss.
  • Exact Absolute Loss in Currency: $$\mathbf{\text{Total Loss (₹)} = \frac{2 \times \text{SP} \times x^2}{100^2 - x^2}}$$ In the above example: Total $\text{SP} = 2 \times 2,40,000 = 4,80,000$.
    Since $4\%$ loss $\implies \text{Total SP}$ is $96\%$ of $\text{Total CP} \implies \text{Total CP} = \frac{480000}{0.96} = 5,00,000$.
    Total Loss $= 5,00,000 - 4,80,000 = \text{₹}20,000$.
  • Equal CP vs Equal SP Distinction: • If two articles are bought at the SAME Cost Price (CP), one sold at $+x\%$ and other at $-x\% \implies \mathbf{\text{No Profit, No Loss (0\%)}}$!
    • If sold at the SAME Selling Price (SP) $\implies \mathbf{\text{ALWAYS Loss of } \frac{x^2}{100}\%}$!
  • Fractional Quantity Split Model: A merchant sells $\frac{2}{3}$ of goods at $15\%$ profit and remaining $\frac{1}{3}$ at $12\%$ loss. What is net profit %?
    Use weighted average: $\text{Net } \% = \frac{2}{3}(+15\%) + \frac{1}{3}(-12\%) = +10\% - 4\% = +6\%$ profit!
🎯 Day 38 Focus: Read carefully whether the two items have Equal CP (net 0%) or Equal SP (net loss of $x^2/100\%$).

📐 Formula Vault

📐 Profit %, Loss % & Multiplier Foundation
Profit% = [(SP - CP)/CP] × 100  |  Loss% = [(CP - SP)/CP] × 100  |  SP = CP × Multiplier
When to use: Base is ALWAYS Cost Price (CP) unless problem explicitly mentions 'profit on SP'. Multiplier: $+20\% \implies 1.20 = 6/5$; $-15\% \implies 0.85 = 17/20$.
⚡ Shortcut: If Profit is $P\%$ on CP, then Profit on $SP = \frac{P}{100 + P} \times 100$. E.g., $25\%$ on CP $= \frac{25}{125} = 20\%$ on SP.
🚨 Trap: Calculating loss% or profit% on Selling Price by default. CP is always denominator unless specified otherwise!
📐 Successive Discount Formula
D_{net} = d_1 + d_2 - \frac{d_1 \times d_2}{100}  |  \text{Three Discounts: } 1 - (1 - d_1)(1 - d_2)(1 - d_3)
When to use: Two or more consecutive percentage reductions on Marked Price (MP).
⚡ Shortcut: For $20\%$ and $10\%$: $20 + 10 - 2 = 28\%$. For three $10\%$ discounts: $100 \to 90 \to 81 \to 72.9 \implies 27.1\%$ net discount.
🚨 Trap: Adding discounts directly ($20\% + 10\% = 30\%$). Successive discounts are always strictly LESS than their sum!
📐 The Golden CP / MP Theorem
\frac{CP}{MP} = \frac{100 - \text{Discount}\%}{100 + \text{Profit}\%}  \text{ (In case of Loss: } \frac{100 - D\%}{100 - L\%}\text{)}
When to use: Any question linking CP, MP, Discount% and Profit%. Eliminates calculating SP entirely!
⚡ Shortcut: Discount $= 10\%$, Profit $= 20\% \implies CP : MP = 90 : 120 = 3 : 4$. Direct $33.33\%$ markup!
🚨 Trap: Swapping numerator and denominator. Remember: Discount reduces MP $\to$ top has $(100-D)$, Profit raises CP $\to$ bottom has $(100+P)$.
📐 Dishonest Dealer & False Weight
\text{Profit}\% = \frac{\text{True Weight} - \text{False Weight}}{\text{False Weight}} \times 100  |  \text{Overall } M = \left(\frac{100+m}{100}\right) \times \left(\frac{\text{True}}{\text{False}}\right)
When to use: Trader claims to sell at CP (or marked up price) but dispenses less quantity.
⚡ Shortcut: Sells $800$g instead of $1000$g: Profit $= \frac{200}{800} \times 100 = 25\%$. Sells at $10\%$ markup and uses $800$g: Net $= \frac{11}{10} \times \frac{1000}{800} = \frac{11}{8} \implies +37.5\%$.
🚨 Trap: Using True Weight (1000g) as denominator. The dealer\'s cost is only for the FALSE weight given!
📐 Buy X Get Y Free Scheme
\text{Discount}\% = \frac{Y}{X + Y} \times 100  |  \text{Combined with } d\% \text{ off: } \left(1 - \frac{Y}{X+Y}\right) \times \left(1 - \frac{d}{100}\right)
When to use: Free article retail promotions ('Buy 3 Get 1 Free', 'Buy 5 Get 2 Free').
⚡ Shortcut: Buy 4 Get 1 Free: $\text{Discount} = \frac{1}{4+1} \times 100 = 20\%$. Buy 5 Get 3 Free: $\frac{3}{8} = 37.5\%$.
🚨 Trap: Dividing by $X$ instead of $(X+Y)$. Total goods leaving the shop is $(X+Y)$, which represents total MP!
📐 Equal SP Theorem (Same SP, $+x\%$ and $-x\%$)
\text{Always a Net LOSS} = \left(\frac{x}{10}\right)^2\% = \frac{x^2}{100}\%  |  \text{Absolute Loss} = \frac{2 \times SP \times x^2}{10000 - x^2}
When to use: Two articles sold at the exact same Selling Price (SP), one at $x\%$ profit and the other at $x\%$ loss.
⚡ Shortcut: Two cars sold at ₹9,900 each, one $+10\%$ and one $-10\%$: Net loss $= 1\%$. Total $SP = 19,800 = 99\% \implies 1\% = ₹200$ loss!
🚨 Trap: Assuming no profit no loss. Equal SP always produces a net LOSS. (If equal CP, then it is 0%).
📐 Article-Price Cross Rule (Quantity vs Rupee)
\text{If CP of } A \text{ articles} = \text{SP of } B \text{ articles} \implies \frac{SP}{CP} = \frac{A}{B} \implies \text{Profit}\% = \frac{A - B}{B} \times 100
When to use: 'CP of 12 items = SP of 9 items' or 'Bought at $x$ for ₹$y$ and sold at $a$ for ₹$b$'.
⚡ Shortcut: Bought 6 for ₹5, sold 5 for ₹6: Cross multiply $\implies CP = 5 \times 5 = 25$, $SP = 6 \times 6 = 36$. Profit $= \frac{11}{25} \times 100 = 44\%$.
🚨 Trap: Using quantity directly as cost. Always invert quantity to price ratio!

⚡ Speed Shortcuts

⚡ 1. Equal SP Shortcut ($(x/10)^2\%$ Rule)
Two items sold at same SP, one at $+x\%$, other at $-x\%$. Overall is ALWAYS a net loss of $(x/10)^2\%$. E.g., $\pm 20\% \implies 4\%$ loss. If total $SP = ₹2,400$, then $CP = 2400 / 0.96 = ₹2,500 \implies$ Loss $= ₹100$ in 5 seconds without finding individual CPs!
⚡ 2. Golden CP/MP 5-Second Ratio Bridge
Whenever Discount% and Profit% are given: $CP : MP = (100 - D) : (100 + P)$. E.g., Discount $= 10\%$, Profit $= 20\% \implies CP : MP = 90 : 120 = 3 : 4$. If $MP = ₹1,200$, then $CP = 3 \times 300 = ₹900$ immediately!
⚡ 3. Cross-Multiplication for "Bought at X for ₹Y, Sold at A for ₹B"
Write [Quantity, Cost]: Bought $[Q_1, C_1]$, Sold $[Q_2, C_2]$. Cross multiply: Effective $CP = Q_2 \times C_1$, Effective $SP = Q_1 \times C_2$. E.g., Bought 8 for ₹10, Sold 6 for ₹12 $\implies CP = 6 \times 10 = 60$, $SP = 8 \times 12 = 96$. Profit $= 36/60 = 60\%$!
⚡ 4. Dishonest Dealer Multiplier Chain
Treat every deception as a ratio multiplier: Markup $+20\% \implies \frac{6}{5}$; gives $800$g for $1000$g $\implies \frac{1000}{800} = \frac{5}{4}$; allows $10\%$ discount $\implies \frac{9}{10}$. Net Multiplier $= \frac{6}{5} \times \frac{5}{4} \times \frac{9}{10} = \frac{27}{20} \implies 7/20 = 35\%$ profit!
⚡ 5. Free Article + Additional Cash Discount
"Buy 4 Get 1 Free" $+ 20\%$ off. First: $1/(4+1) = 20\%$ scheme discount. Combined with $20\%$ cash discount: Net $= 20 + 20 - \frac{400}{100} = 36\%$ total discount. Never add $20 + 20 = 40\%$!
⚡ 6. Weighted Average for Split Inventory
Trader sells $2/3$ of stock at $+15\%$ profit and remaining $1/3$ at $-12\%$ loss. Net Profit $\% = \frac{2}{3}(+15) + \frac{1}{3}(-12) = 10 - 4 = +6\%$. No need to assume total rupees or calculate intermediate values!

🚨 SSC Exam Trap Library

🚨 Trap 1: Dishonest Weight Base Inversion Error-C
A grocer gives 900g instead of 1kg. Many calculate profit as $(100/1000) \times 100 = 10\%$. Wrong! The shopkeeper only incurs cost for 900g. Profit $= \frac{100}{900} \times 100 = 11.11\%$. Always keep False Weight as base.
🚨 Trap 2: Equal SP vs Equal CP Confusion Error-R
Two articles sold at $+20\%$ and $-20\%$. If sold at SAME CP $\implies 0\%$ (No profit, no loss). But if sold at SAME SP $\implies$ ALWAYS a NET LOSS of $(20/10)^2 = 4\%$. Misreading 'SP' as 'CP' causes instant negative marking.
🚨 Trap 3: "Buy X Get Y Free" Denominator Blunder Error-F
"Buy 3 Get 2 Free": Candidates write discount as $2/3 = 66.67\%$. Wrong! The customer receives $3 + 2 = 5$ items total. Marked value $= 5$ units, paid for $= 3$ units $\implies$ Discount $= 2/5 = 40\%$.
🚨 Trap 4: Profit on SP Stated in Question Error-S
SSC states: 'Profit of a salesman is $20\%$ of Selling Price'. Candidates use $Profit\% = 20\%$ on CP. Trap! If $SP = 100$, $Profit = 20 \implies CP = 80$. Actual Profit on $CP = 20/80 = 25\%$. Read bases vigilantly!
🚨 Trap 5: Additive Successive Discounts Error-T
Discounts of $20\%$ and $10\%$ do NOT equal $30\%$. Net discount is $20 + 10 - \frac{200}{100} = 28\%$. Under exam time pressure, students hurriedly sum percentages.
🚨 Trap 6: Discount on CP Instead of MP Error-K
Discount is strictly applied to Marked Price (MP). If an article has $CP = ₹500$ and is marked $20\%$ above CP ($MP = ₹600$), a $10\%$ discount is $₹60$ (on ₹600), NOT $₹50$ (on ₹500). $SP = ₹540$.

Q1: If the Cost Price of $15$ tables is equal to the Selling Price of $20$ tables, find the loss percentage.
Hint: Express as ratio $\frac{SP}{CP} = \frac{15}{20}$. If $SP < CP$, it is a loss.
Answer: $25\%$ Loss.
Step-by-step:
1. $15 \times CP = 20 \times SP \implies \frac{SP}{CP} = \frac{15}{20} = \frac{3}{4}$.
2. Let $CP = 4$ units, then $SP = 3$ units $\implies$ Loss $= 4 - 3 = 1$ unit.
3. $\text{Loss}\% = \frac{1}{4} \times 100 = 25\%$.
Tag if wrong: [C] Arithmetic Ratio Error | [F] Swapped CP and SP

Q2: A trader marks his goods $40\%$ above the Cost Price and allows a discount of $25\%$ on the Marked Price. What is his net profit or loss percentage?
Hint: Use successive percentage formula: $+a - b - \frac{ab}{100}$ where $a = +40$, $b = 25$.
Answer: $5\%$ Profit.
Step-by-step:
1. Successive Formula: Net $\% = +40 - 25 - \frac{40 \times 25}{100} = 15 - 10 = +5\%$.
2. Multiplier Method: $SP = CP \times 1.40 \times 0.75 = CP \times 1.05 \implies +5\%$ gain.
Tag if wrong: [C] Calculation Slip | [F] Formula Sign Error
Level: Moderate SSC CoreThe Golden Formula
Q3: A merchant allows a discount of $12\%$ on the Marked Price and still earns a profit of $32\%$. If the Marked Price of the article is ₹$1,080$, find its Cost Price.
Hint: Golden Formula: $\frac{CP}{MP} = \frac{100 - D\%}{100 + P\%}$. Plug in $D=12, P=32$.
Answer: ₹$720$.
Step-by-step:
1. $\frac{CP}{MP} = \frac{100 - 12}{100 + 32} = \frac{88}{132}$.
2. Divide numerator & denominator by $44$: $\frac{CP}{MP} = \frac{2}{3}$.
3. Since $MP = ₹1,080$ ($3$ units $= 1,080 \implies 1$ unit $= 360$), $CP = 2 \times 360 = ₹720$.
Tag if wrong: [F] Golden formula ratio reversed | [C] Fraction reduction error
Level: Moderate3 Successive Discounts
Q4: What is the single equivalent discount percentage for three successive discounts of $20\%$, $15\%$, and $10\%$?
Hint: Start with ₹100 or multiply surviving factors: $0.80 \times 0.85 \times 0.90$.
Answer: $38.8\%$.
Step-by-step:
1. Combine $20\%$ and $15\%$: $D_{1,2} = 20 + 15 - \frac{300}{100} = 35 - 3 = 32\%$.
2. Combine $32\%$ and $10\%$: $D_{net} = 32 + 10 - \frac{320}{100} = 42 - 3.2 = 38.8\%$.
3. Or multiplier: $1 - (0.8 \times 0.85 \times 0.9) = 1 - 0.612 = 0.388 = 38.8\%$.

Q5: A dishonest shopkeeper claims to sell sugar at Cost Price, but uses a false weight of $920$ grams instead of $1$ kg. Calculate his profit percentage.
Hint: The dealer saves $80$g on every $920$g he actually gives. Base is $920$g!
Answer: $8\frac{16}{23}\% \approx 8.7\%$.
Step-by-step:
1. $\text{Profit}\% = \frac{\text{True Weight} - \text{False Weight}}{\text{False Weight}} \times 100$.
2. $\text{Profit}\% = \frac{1000 - 920}{920} \times 100 = \frac{80}{920} \times 100 = \frac{2}{23} \times 100 = \frac{200}{23} = 8\frac{16}{23}\%$.
Tag if wrong: [C] Used 1000 as base (8%) | [K] Misapplied dishonest dealer formula
Level: Moderate-HighEqual Selling Price
Q6: A person sells two wristwatches for ₹$4,800$ each. On the first watch, he gains $20\%$, and on the second watch, he loses $20\%$. Find his overall gain or loss in Rupees.
Hint: Overall loss is $(20/10)^2 = 4\%$. Total $SP = ₹9,600 = 96\%$ of total CP.
Answer: Loss of ₹$400$.
Step-by-step:
1. Overall Loss $\% = \frac{20^2}{100} = 4\%$.
2. Total $SP = 4,800 + 4,800 = ₹9,600$.
3. Since Loss $= 4\%$, Total $SP = 96\%$ of Total $CP$.
4. $96\% = 9,600 \implies 1\% = 100 \implies 4\% = ₹400$ total loss.

Level: Moderate-HighBuy X Get Y + Extra Discount
Q7: A clothing showroom offers a scheme of 'Buy $5$ Get $3$ Free'. In addition, a customer gets an extra discount of $12.5\%$ on paying via credit card. Find the effective net discount percentage.
Hint: Scheme discount $= 3/(5+3) = 3/8 = 37.5\%$. Additional discount $= 12.5\% = 1/8$. Use multipliers!
Answer: $45.3125\%$ (or $45\frac{5}{16}\%$).
Step-by-step:
1. Free item scheme discount: $d_1 = \frac{3}{5+3} = \frac{3}{8} \implies$ Customer pays $\frac{5}{8}$ of MP.
2. Extra discount: $d_2 = 12.5\% = \frac{1}{8} \implies$ Customer pays $\frac{7}{8}$ of previous price.
3. Net paying fraction: $\frac{5}{8} \times \frac{7}{8} = \frac{35}{64}$.
4. Net Discount $= 1 - \frac{35}{64} = \frac{29}{64} = 45.3125\%$.
Tag if wrong: [F] Used 3/5 as scheme discount | [T] Directly added 37.5 + 12.5 = 50%
Level: ModerateCross Multiplication Rule
Q8: A fruit vendor purchases lemons at the rate of $8$ for ₹$10$ and sells them at the rate of $6$ for ₹$12$. Calculate his overall profit percentage.
Hint: Equalize quantity by finding LCM of $8$ and $6$ ($24$ lemons), or cross-multiply quantity and rupees.
Answer: $60\%$ Profit.
Step-by-step:
1. LCM of $8$ and $6 = 24$ lemons.
2. Cost of $24$ lemons: $\frac{10}{8} \times 24 = ₹30$.
3. Selling price of $24$ lemons: $\frac{12}{6} \times 24 = ₹48$.
4. $\text{Profit} = 48 - 30 = ₹18 \implies \text{Profit}\% = \frac{18}{30} \times 100 = 60\%$.
⚡ Cross Shortcut: $CP = 6 \times 10 = 60$, $SP = 8 \times 12 = 96 \implies \frac{36}{60} \times 100 = 60\%$.
Tag if wrong: [C] Arithmetic multiplication error | [S] Computed per unit incorrectly
Level: High LevelInventory Split / Weighted Avg
Q9: A merchant buys $240$ kg of wheat. He sells $\frac{1}{3}$ of it at a profit of $10\%$, $\frac{1}{4}$ of it at a profit of $20\%$, and the remainder at a loss of $5\%$. Find his overall profit or loss percentage on the entire transaction.
Hint: Remainder fraction $= 1 - (1/3 + 1/4) = 5/12$. Use weighted average of profit percentages!
Answer: $6.25\%$ Profit (or $6\frac{1}{4}\%$).
Step-by-step:
1. Fractions: $\frac{1}{3}$ at $+10\%$, $\frac{1}{4}$ at $+20\%$.
2. Remainder $= 1 - \left(\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4}\right) = 1 - \frac{7}{12} = \frac{5}{12}$ at $-5\%$.
3. Overall Net $\% = \frac{1}{3}(10) + \frac{1}{4}(20) + \frac{5}{12}(-5) = \frac{10}{3} + 5 - \frac{25}{12}$.
4. Common denominator $12$: $\frac{40 + 60 - 25}{12} = \frac{75}{12} = \frac{25}{4} = 6.25\%$ profit.
Tag if wrong: [C] Fraction addition error | [S] Subtracted 5% from 100 instead of net
Level: Advanced SSC Tier-2Double Fraud Dealer
Q10: A fraudulent trader professes to sell his goods at a $10\%$ loss on Cost Price, but uses a false weight which is $25\%$ less than the true weight. What is his actual overall gain or loss percentage?
Hint: Price multiplier $= 0.90$. Weight multiplier $= \frac{100}{100 - 25} = \frac{100}{75} = \frac{4}{3}$. Multiply both!
Answer: $20\%$ Profit.
Step-by-step:
1. Claimed price multiplier: Sells at $10\%$ loss $\implies \frac{SP}{CP} = \frac{90}{100} = \frac{9}{10}$.
2. Weight fraud: Gives $75$g instead of $100$g $\implies$ Multiplier $= \frac{100}{75} = \frac{4}{3}$.
3. Overall Net Multiplier $= \frac{9}{10} \times \frac{4}{3} = \frac{3 \times 2}{5} = \frac{6}{5}$.
4. Since $\frac{SP}{CP} = \frac{6}{5}$, Net Profit $= \frac{6 - 5}{5} \times 100 = \frac{1}{5} \times 100 = 20\%$ profit.
Tag if wrong: [K] Confused weight fraud multiplier direction | [C] Multiplier multiplication slip

SSC CGL 2023 Tier-1 (Shift 2) Difficulty: Easy-Moderate
Question: A shopkeeper marks his goods $35\%$ above the cost price and allows a discount of $20\%$ to the customer. If he earns a total profit of ₹$420$, what is the cost price of the article?
✅ Correct Answer: ₹$5,250

⚡ 15-Second Exam Trick (Successive %):
• Markup $= +35\%$, Discount $= -20\%$.
• Net Profit $\% = +35 - 20 - \frac{35 \times 20}{100} = 15 - 7 = +8\%$.
• We are given: $8\%$ of $CP = ₹420$.
• $1\% = \frac{420}{8} = 52.5 \implies 100\% = 52.5 \times 100 = ₹5,250$.

🚨 Examiner Trap: Students often calculate discount of $20\%$ on CP (taking $35 - 20 = 15\%$ profit) and calculate $420 / 0.15 = ₹2,800$, which is deliberately option A!
SSC CGL 2022 Tier-2 (Mains) Difficulty: Advanced
Question: Two articles are sold for ₹$9,720$ each. On the first article, the seller gains $8\%$, and on the second article, he loses $10\%$. What is his overall gain or loss in rupees?
✅ Correct Answer: Loss of ₹$360

⚡ Step-by-Step SSC Method:
• $SP_1 = ₹9,720$ at $+8\% \implies CP_1 = \frac{9720}{1.08} = ₹9,000$. (Profit $= 9720 - 9000 = +₹720$).
• $SP_2 = ₹9,720$ at $-10\% \implies CP_2 = \frac{9720}{0.90} = ₹10,800$. (Loss $= 10800 - 9720 = -₹1,080$).
• Total Cost Price $= 9,000 + 10,800 = ₹19,800$.
• Total Selling Price $= 9,720 \times 2 = ₹19,440$.
• Overall Result $= 19,440 - 19,800 = -₹360$ (Loss of ₹$360$).

🚨 Examiner Trap: Because percentages differ ($+8\%$ and $-10\%$), you CANNOT use the $x^2/100$ formula! Individual CP calculation via division is mandatory.
SSC CGL 2023 Tier-1 (Shift 1) Difficulty: Moderate
Question: A dishonest dealer sells goods at a profit of $15\%$ on Cost Price, but uses a false weight of $800$g instead of $1$ kg. What is his total percentage profit?
✅ Correct Answer: $43.75\%$ (or $43\frac{3}{4}\%$)

⚡ 10-Second Ratio Multiplier:
• Price profit multiplier $= 1 + \frac{15}{100} = \frac{115}{100} = \frac{23}{20}$.
• Weight fraud multiplier $= \frac{1000}{800} = \frac{5}{4}$.
• Combined Multiplier $= \frac{23}{20} \times \frac{5}{4} = \frac{23}{16}$.
• Profit $\% = \frac{23 - 16}{16} \times 100 = \frac{7}{16} \times 100 = 7 \times 6.25\% = 43.75\%$.

🚨 Examiner Trap: Direct addition of $15\% + 20\% = 35\%$, or calculating weight gain on $1000$g instead of $800$g.
SSC CGL 2021 Tier-2 (Mains) Difficulty: Moderate
Question: By selling an article for ₹$640$, a person loses $20\%$. At what price should he sell it in order to gain $15\%$?
✅ Correct Answer: ₹$920

⚡ Direct Ratio Method (Skip finding CP):
• $SP_1 = 80\%$ of $CP = ₹640 \implies 1\% = \frac{640}{80} = ₹8$.
• Target $SP_2 = 115\%$ of $CP = 115 \times 8 = ₹920$.
• Solved in 8 seconds flat!
SSC CGL 2022 Tier-1 Difficulty: Moderate-High
Question: An article is marked at ₹$800$. After two successive discounts of $x\%$ and $10\%$ on the marked price, it is sold for ₹$612$. Find the value of $x$.
✅ Correct Answer: $x = 15\%

⚡ Reverse Discount Trick:
• Since order of successive discounts doesn't matter, apply $10\%$ first on ₹$800$.
• Intermediate Price $= 800 - 80 = ₹720$.
• Final Price $= ₹612 \implies$ Remaining discount on ₹$720$ is: $720 - 612 = ₹108$.
• $x = \frac{108}{720} \times 100 = \frac{3}{20} \times 100 = 15\%$.

1. What is the Golden Formula connecting CP, MP, Discount% and Profit%?
CP / MP = (100 - Discount%) / (100 + Profit%)
2. Two articles are sold at the same SP: one at +x% profit and the other at -x% loss. What is the overall percentage profit/loss?
Always a NET LOSS of (x/10)^2 % = (x^2 / 100) %.
3. In dishonest dealer false weight problems, which value goes in the denominator of the profit formula?
The FALSE WEIGHT (the actual quantity given to the customer, which represents the dealer's cost).
4. What is the effective discount percentage of 'Buy 3 Get 2 Free'?
Discount% = 2 / (3 + 2) * 100 = 2/5 * 100 = 40%. (Total articles received = 5).

SP / CP = A / B. Profit% = [(A - B) / B] * 100.

SP = 100, Profit = 25 => CP = 75. Actual Profit% = 25/75 * 100 = 33.33% (or 33 1/3 %).

D_net = 20 + 15 - (20 * 15)/100 = 35 - 3 = 32%.
8. If an article is marked up by 30% and then sold at a discount of 10%, what is the net multiplier on CP?
Multiplier = 1.30 * 0.90 = 1.17 => Net profit = +17%.
