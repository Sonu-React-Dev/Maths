📅 Days 27–31 (5 Days) — Day-by-Day Conceptual Master Plan

Day 27
Basic Average & AP Consecutive Series
📝 20 Series Drills
Core Average Formula, Sum Principle & Arithmetic Progression Symmetry
  • Fundamental Definition: $\text{Average (औसत)} = \frac{\text{Sum of all Observations}}{\text{Total Number of Observations}} = \frac{\sum x_i}{N}$.
    • $\mathbf{\text{Total Sum} = \text{Average} \times N}$.
  • Arithmetic Progression (AP) / Consecutive Sequences: • For ANY series in AP with common difference $d$, $\mathbf{\text{Average} = \frac{\text{First Term} + \text{Last Term}}{2}}$.
    • If number of terms $N$ is ODD, Average is the exact middle term.
    • If number of terms $N$ is EVEN, Average is the mean of the two central terms.
  • Standard Series Formulas: • First $n$ natural numbers ($1, 2, 3.. n$): $\text{Avg} = \frac{n+1}{2}$.
    • First $n$ even natural numbers ($2, 4, 6.. 2n$): $\text{Avg} = n + 1$.
    • First $n$ odd natural numbers ($1, 3, 5.. 2n-1$): $\text{Avg} = n$.
    • Squares of first $n$ natural numbers: $\text{Avg} = \frac{(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$.
  • Invariance under Operations: If each observation is increased/decreased/multiplied/divided by constant $k$, the new average changes by the SAME operation: $\bar{X}_{\text{new}} = \bar{X} \pm k$ (or $\bar{X} \times k$, $\bar{X} / k$).
🎯 Day 27 Focus: "Average of 7 consecutive numbers is 20 $\to$ Middle term is 20 $\to$ Largest is $20 + 3 = 23$" (Zero algebra!).
Day 28
Assumed Mean & Weighted Average
📝 25 Deviation Drills
Zero Net Deviation Law & Group Balancing with Alligation
  • The Zero Net Deviation Law: The algebraic sum of deviations of all observations from their true mean is ALWAYS ZERO: $$\mathbf{\sum (x_i - \bar{X}) = 0}$$
  • Assumed Mean Method (कल्पित माध्य): Choose a convenient round number $A$ close to values. Calculate deviation $d_i = x_i - A$. $$\mathbf{\bar{X} = A + \frac{\sum d_i}{N}}$$ Saves massive calculation when numbers are large (e.g. 582, 594, 608, 612).
  • Weighted Average of Two Groups: If group 1 has $n_1$ items with average $A_1$, and group 2 has $n_2$ items with average $A_2$: $$\mathbf{\bar{X}_{\text{comb}} = \frac{n_1 A_1 + n_2 A_2}{n_1 + n_2}}$$ ⚡ Alligation Rule: $\frac{n_1}{n_2} = \frac{A_2 - \bar{X}_{\text{comb}}}{\bar{X}_{\text{comb}} - A_1}$. Ratio of counts depends only on distances from mean!
🎯 Day 28 Focus: Never directly average two averages unless group sizes $n_1 = n_2$ are explicitly equal.
Day 29
Inclusion & Exclusion Models
📝 20 Shift Problems
Teacher Joining, Member Leaving & Overlapping Group Equations
  • Inclusion of a Member: Old average of $N$ items is $A$. One new person joins, new average of $(N+1)$ items becomes $A + \Delta$. $$\mathbf{\text{Value of New Member} = \text{Old Average} + [\text{New Count} \times \Delta]}$$ (If average decreases, $\Delta$ is negative).
  • Exclusion of a Member: A member leaves the group, remaining count is $(N-1)$, average changes by $\Delta$. $$\mathbf{\text{Value of Outgoing Member} = \text{Old Average} - [\text{Remaining Count} \times \Delta]}$$
  • Overlapping Series (Middle Term $M$): Average of $(2n+1)$ items is $A$. First $(n+1)$ average is $A_1$, last $(n+1)$ average is $A_2$. $$\mathbf{\text{Middle Term } (n+1) = [\text{Sum of First } (n+1) + \text{Sum of Last } (n+1)] - \text{Total Sum}}$$ $$\mathbf{M = (n+1)(A_1 + A_2) - (2n+1)A}$$
🎯 Day 29 Focus: Mental deviation: "30 students avg 14, teacher joins avg becomes 15 $\to$ Teacher $= 14 + (31 \times 1) = 45$ years!"
Day 30
Replacement & Cricket Averages
📝 20 Cricket Drills
Replacement Law, Batting Average & Bowling Average Alligation
  • Replacement Formula: When an old member of value $W_{\text{old}}$ is replaced by a new member, the total count $N$ remains CONSTANT: $$\mathbf{W_{\text{new}} = W_{\text{old}} \pm (N \times \Delta)}$$ (Use $+$ if average increases, $-$ if average decreases).
  • Cricket Batting Average: $\text{Batting Avg} = \frac{\text{Total Runs}}{\text{Total Innings Out}}$.
    • To increase average from $A$ to $A + \Delta$ in the $(n+1)^{\text{th}}$ innings:
    $$\mathbf{\text{Runs in } (n+1)^{\text{th}} \text{ innings} = \text{New Average} + [\text{Old Innings } n \times \Delta]}$$
  • Cricket Bowling Average: $\mathbf{\text{Bowling Average} = \frac{\text{Total Runs Conceded}}{\text{Total Wickets Taken}}}$.
    ⚠️ Crucial SSC Trap: When a bowler's average "improves" or "decreases" by 0.4, it means the numerical average becomes SMALLER ($12.4 - 0.4 = 12.0$), because conceding fewer runs per wicket is better bowling!
🎯 Day 30 Focus: Solving bowling average questions using the Alligation method in under 20 seconds.

📐 Formula Vault (7 Master Formulations)

📐 Consecutive Numbers & AP Midpoint Laws
Avg of AP = (First + Last)/2  |  First n Naturals: (n+1)/2  |  Odd: n  |  Even: n+1
Symmetry Law: In any arithmetic progression, terms symmetric from the ends have the same average: $\frac{T_1 + T_n}{2} = \frac{T_2 + T_{n-1}}{2} = \dots = \text{Mean}$.
⚡ Shortcut: If average of $k$ consecutive integers is $A$, the numbers are centered at $A$. If $k$ is odd, $A$ is an integer; if $k$ is even, $A$ ends in $.5$.
🚨 Trap: Confusing sum formulas with average formulas (e.g. Sum of odd numbers is $n^2$, but average is $n$).
📐 The Net Deviation Engine (Assumed Mean)
Σ(xᵢ - X̄) = 0  ⟹  True Mean = A + (Σ dᵢ / N)  [dᵢ = xᵢ - A]
When to use: When given a list of close 3-digit or 4-digit numbers. Pick the round base $A$ nearest to the middle.
⚡ Example: For $58, 62, 65, 71, 54$: Let $A = 60$. Deviations: $-2, +2, +5, +11, -6$. Sum $= +10$. New Avg $= 60 + (10/5) = 62$. Done mentally!
🚨 Trap: Forgetting to divide the sum of deviations by the total count $N$.
📐 Weighted Average & Alligation Rule
X̄_comb = (n₁A₁ + n₂A₂) / (n₁ + n₂)  |  n₁/n₂ = (A₂ - X̄) / (X̄ - A₁)
When to use: Combining classes (Boys & Girls), sections, or mixtures.
⚡ Ratio Substitution: You don't need actual counts $n_1, n_2$; the simplest ratio $n_1:n_2$ gives the exact same combined average!
🚨 Trap: Averaging the averages directly $\frac{A_1 + A_2}{2}$ is mathematically invalid unless $n_1 = n_2$.
📐 Member Inclusion Equation
New Member = Old Avg + (New Count × ΔAvg)  [ΔAvg = New Avg - Old Avg]
When to use: Teacher joins class, new player joins team, new item added.
⚡ Logic: The new member brings their own share of the old average PLUS the extra amount needed to lift every member's score by $\Delta$.
🚨 Trap: Using the OLD count instead of NEW count. The lift $\Delta$ applies to ALL members including the newcomer!
📐 Member Exclusion Equation
Outgoing Member = Old Avg - (Remaining Count × ΔAvg)  [ΔAvg = New Avg - Old Avg]
When to use: A student leaves class, a worker quits, an observation is removed.
⚡ Sign Rule: If average drops after member leaves ($\Delta < 0$), the outgoing member was heavier than average: $\text{Old Avg} - (\text{Count} \times -\Delta) = \text{Old Avg} + (\text{Count} \times |\Delta|)$.
🚨 Trap: Using the initial total count instead of remaining $(N-1)$ count.
📐 Member Replacement Law
New Member = Replaced Member + (N × ΔAvg)  [Count N is CONSTANT]
When to use: One person leaves and another joins simultaneously (total $N$ unchanged).
⚡ 5-Second Solution: $\Delta \times N$ gives the exact difference between the incoming and outgoing person.
🚨 Trap: Adding $1$ to count $N$. In replacement, total count does NOT change!
📐 Cricket Batting & Bowling Average Equations
Runs in (n+1)th Innings = New Avg + (n × ΔAvg)  |  Bowling Avg = Runs / Wickets
Batting: Runs needed $= (n+1)A_{\text{new}} - nA_{\text{old}} = A_{\text{new}} + n(A_{\text{new}} - A_{\text{old}})$.
Bowling: A bowler's average improving by $k$ means new average $= (A_{\text{old}} - k)$. Use Alligation with wickets as weights!
🚨 Trap: Treating bowling average improvement as an addition. In bowling, LOWER runs per wicket is an IMPROVEMENT!

⚡ Speed Shortcuts & Exam Hacks

⚡ 1. The Direct Replacement Delta Hack
"The average weight of 8 persons increases by 2.5 kg when a person of 65 kg is replaced":
• Difference $= N \times \Delta = 8 \times 2.5 = 20$ kg.
• Since average increased, new person is heavier:
$$\text{New Person} = 65 + 20 = \mathbf{85 \text{ kg}}$$! Solved in 3 seconds!
⚡ 2. The Next-Innings Score Mental Formula
"A batsman has an average of 42 runs in 16 innings. How many runs in the 17th innings to raise average to 45?":
• Target average $= 45$.
• Extra runs to distribute to previous 16 innings $= 16 \times (45 - 42) = 16 \times 3 = 48$.
• Required score $= 45 + 48 = \mathbf{93 \text{ runs}}$! No multiplication of $17 \times 45$ needed!
⚡ 3. The Overlapping Middle Number Shortcut
"Average of 11 numbers is 50. First 6 is 49, last 6 is 52":
• Net deviation of first 6: $6 \times (49 - 50) = -6$.
• Net deviation of last 6: $6 \times (52 - 50) = +12$.
• Total net deviation $= -6 + 12 = +6$.
• Middle 6th number $= \text{Base} + \text{Net Deviation} = 50 + 6 = \mathbf{56}$! Zero pencil work!
⚡ 4. Bowling Average Alligation Setup
Previous avg $12.4$, this match performance $= \frac{26 \text{ runs}}{5 \text{ wkts}} = 5.2$. Combined avg $= 12.0$.
$$\begin{matrix} 12.4 & & 5.2 \\ & 12.0 & \\ 6.8 & : & 0.4 \end{matrix}$$ Ratio of wickets $= 6.8 : 0.4 = 17 : 1$.
Since $1 \text{ unit} = 5$ wickets, previous wickets $= 17 \times 5 = 85$. Total wickets $= 85 + 5 = 90$!
⚡ 5. Misread Marks Instant Correction
"Average marks of 40 students was 68. 84 was wrongly entered as 48":
• Error difference $= \text{Correct} - \text{Wrong} = 84 - 48 = +36$.
• Per-student adjustment $= +\frac{36}{40} = +0.9$.
• Correct average $= 68 + 0.9 = \mathbf{68.9}$!
⚡ 6. Consecutive Sequence Extreme Finder
"Average of 9 consecutive odd numbers is 53":
• Middle 5th term is $53$.
• Smallest term $= 53 - 4 \times 2 = 53 - 8 = 45$.
• Largest term $= 53 + 4 \times 2 = 53 + 8 = 61$. (Since odd numbers have step 2).

🚨 SSC Exam Trap Library (6 Verified Traps)

🚨 Trap 1: Averaging Group Averages Directly Error-C
Class A (20 students) avg is 60, Class B (30 students) avg is 80. Students calculate $\frac{60+80}{2} = 70$. FATAL ERROR. Correct weighted avg $= \frac{20(60) + 30(80)}{50} = \frac{1200+2400}{50} = 72$.
🚨 Trap 2: Bowling Average "Improvement" Sign Error-R
A bowler's average of 14 runs/wicket improves by 0.5. Students calculate new average as $14 + 0.5 = 14.5$. WRONG! Improvement means conceding FEWER runs per wicket $\implies 14 - 0.5 = 13.5$.
🚨 Trap 3: Count Confusion in Inclusion vs Replacement Error-S
When a teacher joins 25 students, the new count is 26! But in replacement, count stays 25. Students mix up whether to multiply $\Delta$ by 25 or 26.
🚨 Trap 4: Non-Overlapping Middle Gap Number Error-K
Average of 25 numbers is 18. First 12 avg is 14, last 12 avg is 17. The 13th number is NOT overlapping—it is EXCLUDED: $13^{\text{th}} = \text{Total} - (\text{Sum}_1 + \text{Sum}_2)$. Do not add and subtract in reverse!
🚨 Trap 5: Even Count Consecutive Middle Value Error-F
Average of 6 consecutive integers is 15.5. Students search for integer 15.5. The two middle numbers are 15 and 16! The sequence is $13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18$.
🚨 Trap 6: Misread Marks Sign Reversal Error-C
If correct mark was 84 and wrongly entered as 48, the real marks are HIGHER, so the average must INCREASE ($+0.9$). Students often subtract 0.9 by mistake.

Q7: A cricket bowler's average was 12.4 runs per wicket. In his next match, he took 5 wickets for 26 runs, and thereby his average improved by 0.4. What was the total number of wickets taken by him before this match?
Hint: Improvement means average becomes 12.4 - 0.4 = 12.0. Use Alligation.
Answer: 85 wickets
1. Match performance $= \frac{26 \text{ runs}}{5 \text{ wickets}} = 5.2$ runs/wicket.
2. New overall average $= 12.4 - 0.4 = 12.0$.
3. Set up Alligation:
$$\begin{matrix} \text{Previous} & & \text{Last Match} \\ 12.4 & & 5.2 \\ & 12.0 & \\ (12.0 - 5.2) = 6.8 & : & (12.4 - 12.0) = 0.4 \end{matrix}$$ $$\text{Ratio of Wickets} = 6.8 : 0.4 = 17 : 1$$ Since $1 \text{ unit} = 5$ wickets, previous wickets $= 17 \times 5 = \mathbf{85 \text{ wickets}}$.
(Total wickets after match $= 85 + 5 = 90$).

Q8: The average of 11 numbers is 50. If the average of the first 6 numbers is 49 and that of the last 6 numbers is 52, find the 6th number.
Hint: 6th number is counted in both groups. Sum(first 6) + Sum(last 6) - Total Sum.
Answer: 56
• Fast Deviation Method:
Base average $= 50$.
Deviation of first 6: $6 \times (49 - 50) = -6$.
Deviation of last 6: $6 \times (52 - 50) = +12$.
Net deviation $= -6 + 12 = +6$.
$$6^{\text{th}} \text{ number} = 50 + 6 = \mathbf{56}$$
• Sum Method: $(6 \times 49) + (6 \times 52) - (11 \times 50) = 294 + 312 - 550 = 606 - 550 = 56$.

Q9: The average of 25 numbers is 18. The average of the first 12 numbers is 14 and that of the last 12 numbers is 17. Find the 13th number.
Hint: 13th number is excluded from both groups. Total Sum - (Sum of first 12 + Sum of last 12).
Answer: 78
• Fast Deviation Method:
Deficit in first 12: $12 \times (14 - 18) = -48$.
Deficit in last 12: $12 \times (17 - 18) = -12$.
Total deficit $= -48 - 12 = -60$.
To maintain overall average of 18, the 13th number must supply this deficit:
$$13^{\text{th}} \text{ number} = 18 - (-60) = 18 + 60 = \mathbf{78}$$
• Sum Method: $(25 \times 18) - [(12 \times 14) + (12 \times 17)] = 450 - [168 + 204] = 450 - 372 = 78$.

Q1: The average of 15 numbers is 30. The average of first 8 numbers is 25 and that of last 8 numbers is 32. What is the 8th number?
⚡ Deviation Method (10 Seconds):
Base average $= 30$.
First 8 deviation: $8 \times (25 - 30) = -40$.
Last 8 deviation: $8 \times (32 - 30) = +16$.
Net deviation $= -40 + 16 = -24$.
$$8^{\text{th}} \text{ number} = 30 + (-24) = \mathbf{6}$$
Answer: 6
SSC CGL 2022 Tier-2 (Mains) • Verified PYQ
Q2: A batsman in his 13th innings scores 97 runs and thereby increases his score average by 5. What is his average score after the 13th innings?
⚡ 5-Second Cricket Shortcut:
Extra runs distributed to previous 12 innings $= 12 \times 5 = 60$.
New average after 13th innings $= 97 - 60 = \mathbf{37 \text{ runs}}$.
Answer: 37
SSC CGL 2022 Tier-1 • Verified PYQ
Q3: The average weight of 3 men A, B, and C is 84 kg. Another man D joins them and the average now becomes 80 kg. If another man E, whose weight is 3 kg more than that of D, replaces A, the average weight of B, C, D, and E becomes 79 kg. What is the weight of A?
⚡ Sequential Inclusion-Replacement Method:
1. When D joins (A, B, C, D):
$$D = 84 + [4 \times (80 - 84)] = 84 - 16 = 68 \text{ kg}$$ 2. $E = D + 3 = 68 + 3 = 71 \text{ kg}$.
3. E replaces A in (B, C, D, E): Average drops from 80 to 79 ($\Delta = -1$):
$$E - A = N \times \Delta = 4 \times (79 - 80) = -4 \implies A = E + 4$$ $$A = 71 + 4 = \mathbf{75 \text{ kg}}$$
Answer: 75 kg
SSC CGL 2021 Tier-2 (Mains) • Verified PYQ
Q4: The average score of 42 students in a test is 69. The ratio of the number of boys to girls is $10:11$. The average score of boys is 20% more than that of girls. What is the average score of boys?
⚡ Ratio Multiplier Shortcut:
Ratio of counts: Boys : Girls $= 10 : 11$.
Let average score of girls $= 5x$. Then boys $= 6x$ ($20\%$ more).
Combined average:
$$\frac{10(6x) + 11(5x)}{10 + 11} = 69$$ $$\frac{60x + 55x}{21} = 69 \implies \frac{115x}{21} = 69$$ Divide 115 and 69 by 23: $\frac{5x}{21} = 3 \implies 5x = 63 \implies x = 12.6$.
Average score of boys $= 6x = 6 \times 12.6 = \mathbf{75.6}$.

Q5: In an exam, the average marks obtained by 50 students was 65. Later, it was found that the marks of one student were wrongly entered as 83 instead of 38. What is the correct average?
⚡ 5-Second Correction:
Error difference $= 38 - 83 = -45$.
Adjustment per student $= -\frac{45}{50} = -0.9$.
Correct average $= 65 - 0.9 = \mathbf{64.1}$.
Answer: 64.1
