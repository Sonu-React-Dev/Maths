📅 Days 79–83 (5 Days) — Day-by-Day Conceptual Master Plan

Day 79
2D Figures — Triangles & Quadrilaterals
📝 25 Area/Perimeter Drills
Heron's Formula, Rhombus Diagonals, Trapezium & Path Border Formulas
  • Triangle Area Formulas: • Standard: $\mathbf{\Delta = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Base} \times \text{Height}}$.
    • Trigonometric: $\mathbf{\Delta = \frac{1}{2}ab\sin\theta}$ (Area enclosed by sides $a$ and $b$ with angle $\theta$).
    • Heron's Formula: $\mathbf{\Delta = \sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}}$ where semi-perimeter $s = \frac{a+b+c}{2}$.
  • Quadrilateral Suite: • Rectangle: $\text{Area} = lb$, $\text{Perimeter} = 2(l+b)$, $\text{Diagonal} = \sqrt{l^2 + b^2}$.
    • Square: $\text{Area} = a^2 = \frac{d^2}{2}$, $\text{Perimeter} = 4a$, $\text{Diagonal} = a\sqrt{2}$.
    • Rhombus (समचतुर्भुज): $\mathbf{\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} d_1 d_2}$. Diagonals bisect at $90^\circ \implies \mathbf{4a^2 = d_1^2 + d_2^2}$.
    • Parallelogram: $\text{Area} = \text{Base} \times \text{Height} = ab\sin\theta$.
    • Trapezium (समलंब): $\mathbf{\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(a + b) \times h}$ (Half sum of parallel sides $\times$ height).
  • Path Surrounding a Rectangle ($l \times b$ with path width $w$): • Outside Path: $\mathbf{\text{Area} = 2w(l + b + 2w)}$.
    • Inside Path: $\mathbf{\text{Area} = 2w(l + b - 2w)}$.
    • Crossroads at Center: $\mathbf{\text{Area} = w(l + b - w)}$.
🎯 Day 79 Focus: For Rhombus, side $a = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{d_1^2 + d_2^2}$. Use Pythagorean triplets on half-diagonals to find side in 3 seconds!
Day 80
Circles, Sectors, Rings & Shaded Regions
📝 25 Arc/Sector Drills
Semicircle Perimeter Trap, Sector Formula & Leaf/Corner Circle Theorems
  • Circle & Semicircle Metrics: • Circle: $\text{Area} = \pi r^2$, $\text{Circumference} = 2\pi r$.
    • 🚨 Semicircle Perimeter Trap: $\mathbf{P = \pi r + 2r = \frac{36}{7}r}$ (NOT just $\pi r$!).
  • Sector & Arc Length Formulas: • Arc Length ($l$): $\mathbf{l = \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} \times 2\pi r}$.
    • Sector Area: $\mathbf{\text{Area} = \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2 = \frac{1}{2} l r}$. (Half arc length $\times$ radius).
  • Circular Ring (Annulus / वृत्ताकार वलय): $$\mathbf{\text{Area} = \pi(R^2 - r^2) = \pi(R + r)(R - r)}$$ If length of chord of outer circle touching inner circle is $2L$, then $\text{Area of Ring} = \mathbf{\pi L^2}$.
  • Classical SSC Shaded Area Patterns: • Square of side $a$ with 4 corner quarter circles (Grazing area left un-grazed):
    $\mathbf{\text{Unshaded Area} = a^2 - \pi\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)^2 = a^2\left(1 - \frac{\pi}{4}\right) \approx \frac{3}{14}a^2}$.
    • Leaf Pattern (Two quarter circle overlaps): $\mathbf{\text{Area of Leaf} = \frac{4}{7}a^2}$.
🎯 Day 80 Focus: Semicircle perimeter is $\frac{36}{7}r$. Shaded leaf area in square is $\frac{4}{7}a^2$. Memorize these ratios directly.
Day 81
3D Prisms — Cube, Cuboid & Cylinder
📝 25 Surface/Volume Qs
Longest Rod in Room, Hollow Pipes & Diagonal Invariants
  • Cuboid ($l \times b \times h$): • $\mathbf{\text{Volume} = l \times b \times h}$.
    • $\mathbf{\text{Total Surface Area (TSA)} = 2(lb + bh + hl)}$.
    • $\mathbf{\text{Lateral Surface Area (Area of 4 walls)} = 2(l + b)h}$.
    • $\mathbf{\text{Longest Diagonal (Longest rod that fits)} = \sqrt{l^2 + b^2 + h^2}}$.
    ⚡ Sum of Dimensions Shortcut: $(l + b + h)^2 = (l^2 + b^2 + h^2) + 2(lb + bh + hl) = (\text{Diag})^2 + \text{TSA}$.
  • Cube (Side $a$): $\text{Volume} = a^3$  |  $\text{TSA} = 6a^2$  |  $\text{LSA} = 4a^2$  |  $\text{Diagonal} = a\sqrt{3}$.
  • Right Circular Cylinder (Radius $r$, Height $h$): • $\mathbf{\text{Volume} = \pi r^2 h}$.
    • $\mathbf{\text{Curved Surface Area (CSA)} = 2\pi rh}$.
    • $\mathbf{\text{Total Surface Area (TSA)} = 2\pi r(r + h)}$.
  • Hollow Cylinder (Pipes with outer $R$, inner $r$): $\mathbf{\text{Volume} = \pi(R^2 - r^2)h}$  |  $\mathbf{\text{TSA} = 2\pi(R + r)h + 2\pi(R^2 - r^2)}$.
🎯 Day 81 Focus: Length of longest rod that fits in a cuboidal room is ALWAYS $\sqrt{l^2 + b^2 + h^2}$.
Day 82
3D Pyramids — Cone, Sphere & Frustum
📝 25 Solid Geometry Qs
Slant Height Triplet, Hemisphere TSA & Truncated Cone Frustum
  • Right Circular Cone ($r, h, l$): • Slant Height: $\mathbf{l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}}$. (Pythagorean triplet with $r$ and $h$!).
    • $\mathbf{\text{Volume} = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h}$  |  $\mathbf{\text{CSA} = \pi r l}$  |  $\mathbf{\text{TSA} = \pi r(r + l)}$.
  • Sphere & Hemisphere: • Sphere: $\mathbf{\text{Volume} = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3}$  |  $\mathbf{\text{Surface Area} = 4\pi r^2}$.
    • Hemisphere: $\mathbf{\text{Volume} = \frac{2}{3}\pi r^3}$  |  $\mathbf{\text{CSA} = 2\pi r^2}$  |  🚨 TSA $= 3\pi r^2$! (Includes base circle $\pi r^2$).
  • Frustum of a Cone (Bucket shape, Radii $R$ and $r$): • Slant Height: $\mathbf{l = \sqrt{h^2 + (R - r)^2}}$.
    • $\mathbf{\text{Volume} = \frac{1}{3}\pi h (R^2 + r^2 + Rr)}$.
    • $\mathbf{\text{CSA} = \pi (R + r) l}$  |  $\mathbf{\text{TSA} = \pi (R + r) l + \pi R^2 + \pi r^2}$.
🎯 Day 82 Focus: Hemisphere Total Surface Area is $3\pi r^2$, not $2\pi r^2$.
Day 83
Recasting, Melting & Phase 12 Timed Test
📝 25 Qs in 22 Minutes
Volume Invariance Law, Water-Rise Physics & The 11-Divisibility Check
  • The Law of Conservation of Volume (Recasting): When solid objects are melted and recast into a different shape, $\mathbf{\text{Total Volume}_{\text{initial}} = \text{Total Volume}_{\text{final}}}$ (unless loss is specified).
  • Number of Spherical Balls: $$\mathbf{N = \frac{\text{Volume of Big Object}}{\text{Volume of 1 Small Ball}} = \frac{\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3}{\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3} = \left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^3}$$
  • Water Rise in a Cylinder/Tank: When a solid body is dropped into a liquid container:
    $$\mathbf{\text{Volume of Submerged Body} = \text{Base Area of Container} \times \text{Rise in Water Level } (h)}$$
  • The 11-Divisibility Filter for Answers with $\pi$: Since $\pi = \frac{22}{7} = \frac{2 \times 11}{7}$, any cylinder, cone, sphere area/volume MUST be a multiple of $11$ (unless radius contains a multiple of 7). Filter options in 8 seconds!
  • Phase 12 Timed Mastery Protocol: 25 mixed Mensuration questions under 22-minute timer. Target: $\ge 85\%$ accuracy.
🎯 Day 83 Focus: Check 11-divisibility on options first before solving lengthy $\pi$ formulas.

📐 Formula Vault

📐 2D Polygons, Rhombus, Trapezium & Equilateral $\Delta$
\text{Rhombus: } \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}d_1 d_2, \; 4a^2 = d_1^2 + d_2^2  |  \text{Trapezium: } \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h  |  \text{Eq } \Delta: \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2, \; r = \frac{a}{2\sqrt{3}}, \; R = \frac{a}{\sqrt{3}}
When to use: Calculating areas, inradius, and circumradius of 2D geometric shapes.
⚡ Shortcut: Rhombus side: Half-diagonals $(d_1/2, d_2/2)$ and side $a$ form a right-angled triangle. E.g. $d_1=16, d_2=12 \implies$ half-diagonals $8, 6 \implies a = 10$ cm (via $6:8:10$ triplet)!
🚨 Trap: Rhombus perimeter is $4a$, NOT $2(d_1 + d_2)$. Diagonals do NOT equal sides!
📐 Cylinder, Hollow Cylinder & Wire Drawing
V = \pi r^2 h  |  \text{CSA} = 2\pi rh  |  \text{TSA} = 2\pi r(r + h)  |  \text{Hollow } V = \pi(R^2 - r^2)h
When to use: Cylinders, pipes, coins melted/stacked, and metallic rods drawn into wires.
⚡ Shortcut: Melting rod into wire: Volume is conserved: $\pi r_{\text{rod}}^2 L_{\text{rod}} = \pi r_{\text{wire}}^2 L_{\text{wire}}$. Ensure length and radius units match (cm to m)!
🚨 Trap: Hollow cylinder TSA must include the two circular ring bases at the ends ($2\pi(R^2 - r^2)$).
📐 Cone, Frustum & Slant Height Relations
l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}  |  V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h  |  \text{CSA} = \pi r l  |  \text{Frustum } V = \frac{1}{3}\pi h(R^2 + r^2 + Rr)
When to use: Conical tents, buckets, heaps of sand, and cutting cones with parallel planes.
⚡ Shortcut: In $\text{CSA} = \pi r l$, remember $l$ is the SLANT height, formed by Pythagorean triplet $(r, h, l)$. For $r=12, h=16 \implies l=20$ cm instantly!
🚨 Trap: Using vertical height $h$ in CSA instead of slant height $l$. CSA depends strictly on the hypotenuse slant edge!
📐 Sphere, Hemisphere & Spherical Shells
\text{Sphere: } V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3, \; SA = 4\pi r^2  |  \text{Hemisphere: } V = \frac{2}{3}\pi r^3, \; \text{CSA} = 2\pi r^2, \; \text{TSA} = 3\pi r^2
When to use: Spheres, hemispheres, lead shots, bowls, and hemispherical domes.
⚡ Shortcut: Solid hemisphere Total Surface Area $= 3\pi r^2$ ($2\pi r^2$ curved surface $+$ $\pi r^2$ flat circular base).
🚨 Trap: Writing $2\pi r^2$ for hemisphere TSA. Curved is $2\pi r^2$, but TOTAL is $3\pi r^2$!
📐 Right Prism & Pyramid Fundamental Metrics
\text{Prism: } V = A_{\text{base}} \times H, \; \text{LSA} = P_{\text{base}} \times H  |  \text{Pyramid: } V = \frac{1}{3} A_{\text{base}} \times H, \; \text{LSA} = \frac{1}{2} P_{\text{base}} \times l
When to use: Prisms (triangular, hexagonal bases) and pyramids with polygon bases.
⚡ Shortcut: Regular Tetrahedron (edge $a$): Height $= a\sqrt{2/3}$, Volume $= \frac{a^3}{6\sqrt{2}}$, $\text{TSA} = \sqrt{3}a^2$.
🚨 Trap: In pyramid LSA, use SLANT HEIGHT ($l$), NOT vertical height ($H$). Slant height connects apex to midpoint of base edge!
📐 Recasting, Melting & Number of Castings
n = \frac{\text{Volume}_{\text{large}}}{\text{Volume}_{\text{small}}}  |  \text{Spheres: } n = \left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^3  |  \text{With Wastage: } n \cdot V_{\text{sm}} = V_{\text{lg}}\left(1 - \frac{w}{100}\right)
When to use: Melting a large metal shape to form smaller solid objects.
⚡ Shortcut: Large sphere of radius $R=6$ melted into spheres of $r=0.5$: $n = (6/0.5)^3 = 12^3 = 1,728$ in 3 seconds!
🚨 Trap: Forgetting wastage reduction factor $(1 - w/100)$ when problem mentions melting loss.
📐 Paths, Borders & Room Diagonals
\text{Outer Path} = 2w(l + b + 2w)  |  \text{Inner Path} = 2w(l + b - 2w)  |  \text{Longest Rod} = \sqrt{l^2 + b^2 + h^2}
When to use: Walking paths around lawns, crossroads inside parks, and longest pole inside a room.
⚡ Shortcut: Longest rod inside room $(12 \times 9 \times 8)$: $\sqrt{144 + 81 + 64} = \sqrt{289} = 17$ m instantly!
🚨 Trap: Crossroad path area has $-w$ at the end because the center square of width $w \times w$ overlaps and must be subtracted once!

⚡ Speed Shortcuts

⚡ 1. Divisibility by 11 (The $\pi$ Elimination Hack)
Every formula with $\pi = 22/7$ contains a factor of $11$. Check option choices for divisibility by $11$: $(Sum_{\text{odd digits}} - Sum_{\text{even digits}}) = 0$ or multiple of $11$. Often eliminates 3 wrong options in 5 seconds! (Verify radius doesn't have a 7-factor in denominator).
⚡ 2. Percentage Multipliers in Volume & Area
Radius increases by $+20\%$, height decreases by $-15\%$: Volume factor for cylinder/cone $= (1.2)^2 \times 0.85 = 1.44 \times 0.85 = 1.224 \implies +22.4\%$ net increase without assuming numerical values!
⚡ 3. Rhombus 5-Second Side from Triplet
Diagonals bisect at $90^\circ$. Half-diagonals are legs of right triangle. If $d_1 = 24, d_2 = 10 \implies$ legs are $12$ and $5 \implies$ side $a = 13$ cm (via $5:12:13$ triplet). Perimeter $= 4 \times 13 = 52$ cm!
⚡ 4. Longest Rod in a Room Space Diagonal
Longest pole that fits in a cuboidal room is the space diagonal: $D = \sqrt{l^2 + b^2 + h^2}$. E.g., for $12 \text{ m} \times 9 \text{ m} \times 8 \text{ m}$: $\sqrt{144 + 81 + 64} = \sqrt{289} = 17$ m!
⚡ 5. Cutting a Cone Parallel to Base
A cone of height $H$ is cut at distance $h$ from apex: Volume ratio is $\left(\frac{h}{H}\right)^3$. If cut at $1/3$ height from top ($h/H = 1/3$): Small cone $= (1/3)^3 = 1/27$. Remaining Frustum $= 1 - 1/27 = 26/27$. Ratio of small cone to frustum $= 1 : 26$!
⚡ 6. Sphere-to-Small Spheres Ratio
Number of spheres of radius $r$ formed from large sphere of radius $R$: $n = \left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^3$. For $R = 15$ cm and $r = 3$ cm $\implies n = (15/3)^3 = 5^3 = 125$ spheres in 2 seconds!

🚨 SSC Exam Trap Library

🚨 Trap 1: Hemisphere TSA Base Omission Error-F
Hemisphere CSA $= 2\pi r^2$. But Total Surface Area (TSA) includes the flat circular base ($ op = \pi r^2$), making it $3\pi r^2$! Using $2\pi r^2$ for TSA is the #1 mensuration mistake in SSC exams.
🚨 Trap 2: Cone Slant Height vs Height Error-C
Cone CSA $= \pi r l$ where $l$ is the SLANT height: $l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}$. Candidates mistakenly plug in the vertical height $h$ instead of $l$, yielding incorrect CSA.
🚨 Trap 3: Recasting Material Wastage Ignored Error-R
If $10\%$ material is lost during melting, the effective usable volume is $0.90 \times V_{\text{large}}$. Dividing total initial volume without deducting wastage produces an inflated number of small castings.
🚨 Trap 4: Inner vs Outer Path Formula Sign Error-K
Outer path around a lawn adds width: $2w(l + b + 2w)$. Inner path running inside a lawn subtracts width: $2w(l + b - 2w)$. Swapping $(+)$ and $(-)$ ruins the answer.
🚨 Trap 5: Hollow Cylinder End Rings Omission Error-S
Hollow cylinder TSA $= 2\pi Rh + 2\pi rh + 2\pi(R^2 - r^2)$. Candidates sum internal and external curved surfaces but forget the two flat circular washer rings at the top and bottom.
🚨 Trap 6: Frustum Slant Height Sign Trap Error-T
Frustum slant height $l = \sqrt{h^2 + (R - r)^2}$. Candidates frequently add radii as $(R + r)^2$ inside the square root, creating an impossibly long slant edge.

💡 Guided Practice Questions (One at a Time)

Question 1 of 10
Level: Basic FoundationRhombus Metrics
Q1: The diagonals of a rhombus are $24$ cm and $10$ cm. Find its area, the length of its side, and its perimeter.
Hint: Area $= \frac{1}{2}d_1 d_2$. Side $a = \sqrt{(d_1/2)^2 + (d_2/2)^2} = \sqrt{12^2 + 5^2}$.
Answer: Area $= 120\text{ cm}^2$; Side $= 13$ cm; Perimeter $= 52$ cm.
Step-by-step:
1. Area $= \frac{1}{2} \times 24 \times 10 = 120\text{ cm}^2$.
2. Half-diagonals are $12$ cm and $5$ cm.
3. Side $a = \sqrt{12^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{144 + 25} = \sqrt{169} = 13$ cm.
4. Perimeter $= 4a = 4 \times 13 = 52$ cm.
Tag if wrong: [F] Used (d1+d2) for perimeter | [C] Arithmetic error
Level: FoundationTrapezium Area
Q2: The parallel sides of a trapezium are $18$ cm and $24$ cm, and the perpendicular distance between them is $12$ cm. Find its area.
Hint: $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)h = \frac{1}{2}(18 + 24) \times 12$.
Answer: $252\text{ cm}^2$.
Step-by-step:
1. $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times (18 + 24) \times 12 = \frac{1}{2} \times 42 \times 12$.
2. $\text{Area} = 21 \times 12 = 252\text{ cm}^2$.
Tag if wrong: [C] Arithmetic multiplication error | [F] Forgot the 1/2 factor
Level: Moderate SSC CoreEquilateral Triangle Inradius & Circumradius
Q3: An equilateral triangle has side $12$ cm. Find its area, inradius ($r$), and circumradius ($R$).
Hint: Area $= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2$. $r = \frac{a}{2\sqrt{3}}$, $R = \frac{a}{\sqrt{3}} = 2r$.
Answer: Area $= 36\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}^2$; $r = 2\sqrt{3}$ cm; $R = 4\sqrt{3}$ cm.
Step-by-step:
1. Area $= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \times 12^2 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \times 144 = 36\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}^2$.
2. Inradius $r = \frac{12}{2\sqrt{3}} = \frac{6}{\sqrt{3}} = 2\sqrt{3}$ cm.
3. Circumradius $R = 2 \times r = 4\sqrt{3}$ cm (or $\frac{12}{\sqrt{3}} = 4\sqrt{3}$ cm).
Tag if wrong: [F] Swapped r and R formulas | [C] Surd rationalization error
Level: ModerateSolid Cylinder Metrics
Q4: A solid right circular cylinder has base radius $7$ cm and height $20$ cm. Find its volume, Curved Surface Area (CSA), and Total Surface Area (TSA) using $\pi = 22/7$.
Hint: $V = \pi r^2 h$. $\text{CSA} = 2\pi rh$. $\text{TSA} = 2\pi r(r + h) = \text{CSA} + 2\pi r^2$.
Answer: Volume $= 3,080\text{ cm}^3$; CSA $= 880\text{ cm}^2$; TSA $= 1,188\text{ cm}^2$.
Step-by-step:
1. Volume $= \frac{22}{7} \times 7^2 \times 20 = 22 \times 7 \times 20 = 154 \times 20 = 3,080\text{ cm}^3$.
2. $\text{CSA} = 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 20 = 44 \times 20 = 880\text{ cm}^2$.
3. Base areas $= 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 49 = 308\text{ cm}^2 \implies \text{TSA} = 880 + 308 = 1,188\text{ cm}^2$.
Tag if wrong: [C] Arithmetic error | [F] TSA omitted top or bottom circle
Level: Moderate-HighCone Slant Height & CSA
Q5: The base radius of a right circular cone is $12$ cm and its vertical height is $16$ cm. Find its slant height and Curved Surface Area (CSA) in terms of $\pi$.
Hint: $l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2} = \sqrt{12^2 + 16^2} = 20$ cm (via $3:4:5$ triplet scaled by 4). $\text{CSA} = \pi r l$.
Answer: Slant height $= 20$ cm; CSA $= 240\pi\text{ cm}^2$.
Step-by-step:
1. Slant height $l = \sqrt{12^2 + 16^2} = \sqrt{144 + 256} = \sqrt{400} = 20$ cm.
2. $\text{CSA} = \pi r l = \pi \times 12 \times 20 = 240\pi\text{ cm}^2$.
Tag if wrong: [C] Used vertical height 16 in CSA (getting 192 pi) | [F] Slant height formula error
Level: Moderate SSC CoreSolid Hemisphere TSA
Q6: Find the Total Surface Area of a solid hemisphere of radius $7$ cm (use $\pi = 22/7$).
Hint: Solid hemisphere TSA $= 3\pi r^2 = 3 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 7^2$. Do NOT use $2\pi r^2$!
Answer: $462\text{ cm}^2$.
Step-by-step:
1. Formula for solid hemisphere TSA $= 3\pi r^2$.
2. $\text{TSA} = 3 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 = 3 \times 22 \times 7 = 66 \times 7 = 462\text{ cm}^2$.
Tag if wrong: [F] Used 2*pi*r^2 = 308 cm^2 (CSA trap) | [C] Multiplication error
Level: Moderate-HighRecasting Sphere to Cones
Q7: A solid metallic sphere of radius $10.5$ cm is melted and recast into small solid cones, each of radius $3.5$ cm and height $3$ cm. Find the number of cones formed.
Hint: $n = \frac{\text{Volume of sphere}}{\text{Volume of cone}} = \frac{\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3}{\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h} = \frac{4 R^3}{r^2 h}$.
Answer: $126$ cones.
Step-by-step:
1. Formula: $n = \frac{4 R^3}{r^2 h}$.
2. Note: $R = 10.5 = \frac{21}{2}$, $r = 3.5 = \frac{7}{2}$, $h = 3$.
3. $\frac{R}{r} = \frac{10.5}{3.5} = 3$.
4. $n = \frac{4 \times (10.5)^3}{(3.5)^2 \times 3} = \frac{4 \times 10.5 \times (10.5/3.5)^2}{3} = \frac{4 \times 10.5 \times 9}{3} = 4 \times 10.5 \times 3 = 42 \times 3 = 126$ cones.
Tag if wrong: [C] Arithmetic simplification error | [F] Forgot the 4 factor for sphere volume
Level: Moderate-HighWire Drawing from Rod
Q8: A solid copper cylindrical rod of diameter $1$ cm and length $8$ cm is drawn into a wire of length $18$ m of uniform circular cross-section. Find the radius of the wire.
Hint: Convert 18 m to cm: $1800$ cm. $\pi r_1^2 h_1 = \pi r_2^2 h_2$. $r_1 = 0.5$ cm.
Answer: $\frac{1}{30}$ cm (or $0.33$ mm).
Step-by-step:
1. Rod: Radius $r_1 = 0.5 = \frac{1}{2}$ cm, Length $h_1 = 8$ cm.
2. Volume of rod $= \pi \times \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 \times 8 = \pi \times \frac{1}{4} \times 8 = 2\pi\text{ cm}^3$.
3. Wire: Length $h_2 = 18\text{ m} = 1,800$ cm. Volume $= \pi r_2^2 \times 1,800$.
4. Equate volumes: $2\pi = \pi r_2^2 \times 1800 \implies r_2^2 = \frac{2}{1800} = \frac{1}{900}$.
5. Taking square root: $r_2 = \frac{1}{30}$ cm.
Tag if wrong: [T] Unit conversion error (18 m to cm) | [C] Squaring radius error
Level: High LevelFrustum Bucket Capacity
Q9: A bucket is in the form of a frustum of a cone with top circular radius $20$ cm, bottom circular radius $10$ cm, and vertical depth $14$ cm. Find its capacity in litres (use $\pi = 22/7$).
Hint: $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi h (R^2 + r^2 + Rr) = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 14 \times (400 + 100 + 200)$. $1000\text{ cm}^3 = 1$ litre.
Answer: $10.267$ litres (or $10\frac{4}{15}$ litres).
Step-by-step:
1. Frustum Volume $= \frac{1}{3}\pi h(R^2 + r^2 + Rr)$.
2. Bracket $= 20^2 + 10^2 + (20 \times 10) = 400 + 100 + 200 = 700\text{ cm}^2$.
3. $V = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 14 \times 700 = \frac{1}{3} \times 22 \times 2 \times 700 = \frac{30,800}{3} = 10,266.67\text{ cm}^3$.
4. Convert to litres: $\frac{10,266.67}{1,000} \approx 10.267$ litres.
Tag if wrong: [F] Used (R+r)^2 instead of R^2 + r^2 + Rr | [T] Litre conversion slip
Level: Advanced Tier-2Cone Volume Cut by Parallel Plane
Q10: A solid right circular cone of height $30$ cm is sliced by a plane parallel to its base at a distance of $10$ cm from its apex. Find the ratio of the volume of the smaller cone so formed to the volume of the remaining frustum.
Hint: Ratio of heights is $10 : 30 = 1 : 3$. Volume ratio of smaller cone to original cone is $(1/3)^3 = 1 : 27$. Frustum $= 27 - 1 = 26$.
Answer: $1 : 26$.
Step-by-step:
1. Height ratio $\frac{h}{H} = \frac{10}{30} = \frac{1}{3}$.
2. Since smaller cone is similar to original cone, Volume ratio $= \left(\frac{h}{H}\right)^3 = \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^3 = \frac{1}{27}$.
3. Let original cone volume $= 27$ units. Smaller cone volume $= 1$ unit.
4. Volume of remaining frustum $= 27 - 1 = 26$ units.
5. Ratio of smaller cone to frustum $= 1 : 26$.
Tag if wrong: [S] Answered 1:27 (ratio to whole cone instead of frustum) | [F] Squared instead of cubing

Question: If the radius of a cylinder is increased by $20\%$ and its height is decreased by $15\%$, find the percentage change in its volume.
✅ Correct Answer: Increases by $22.4\%$

⚡ 10-Second Multiplier Shortcut:
• Volume of cylinder $\propto r^2 h$.
• $r$ factor $= 1.20 \implies r^2$ factor $= 1.20^2 = 1.44$.
• $h$ factor $= 1 - 0.15 = 0.85$.
• Net Volume Multiplier $= 1.44 \times 0.85 = 1.224$.
• Percentage change $= (1.224 - 1) \times 100 = +22.4\%$ (Increase).

🚨 Examiner Trap: Adding percentage changes directly: $20 - 15 = 5\%$. In volume, radius appears TWICE ($r^2$), so its compounding effect is dominant!

Question: A right circular cylinder has diameter $14$ cm and volume $1,540\text{ cm}^3$. What is its curved surface area in $\text{cm}^2$?
✅ Correct Answer: $440\text{ cm}^2$

• Radius $r = 14/2 = 7$ cm.

• Height $h = \frac{\text{Volume}}{\text{Base Area}} = \frac{1540}{154} = 10$ cm.

SSC CGL 2022 Tier-1 Difficulty: Moderate-High
Question: The base of a right prism is an equilateral triangle of side $8$ cm, and its height is $10$ cm. Find the total surface area of the prism.
✅ Correct Answer: $(240 + 32\sqrt{3})\text{ cm}^2$

⚡ Step-by-Step Prism TSA:
• Base perimeter $= 3 \times 8 = 24$ cm.
• $\text{LSA} = \text{Base Perimeter} \times \text{Height} = 24 \times 10 = 240\text{ cm}^2$.
• Area of $1$ base $= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \times 8^2 = 16\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}^2$.
• Area of $2$ bases $= 2 \times 16\sqrt{3} = 32\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}^2$.
• $\text{TSA} = \text{LSA} + 2(\text{Base Area}) = 240 + 32\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}^2$.

1. What is the Total Surface Area (TSA) formula of a solid hemisphere of radius r?
TSA = 3 * pi * r² (2*pi*r² curved surface + pi*r² flat circular base).
2. How do you find the side of a rhombus from its two diagonals d1 and d2?
Side a = sqrt((d1/2)² + (d2/2)²) or 4a² = d1² + d2².
3. What is the formula for the length of the longest rod that can be placed in a room of dimensions l, b, h?
Longest rod = sqrt(l² + b² + h²).
4. What are the formulas for inradius (r) and circumradius (R) of an equilateral triangle of side a?
Inradius r = a / (2*sqrt(3)). Circumradius R = a / sqrt(3) = 2r.
5. If a cone of height H is cut by a parallel plane at height h from the apex, what is the ratio of volumes of smaller cone to original cone?
Volume ratio = (h / H)³.
6. What is the volume formula for a frustum of a cone with radii R, r and height h?
V = (1/3) * pi * h * (R² + r² + R*r).
7. What are the LSA and Volume formulas for a right prism with base perimeter P and base area A?
Volume = Base Area * Height. LSA = Base Perimeter * Height. TSA = LSA + 2*(Base Area).

Check for divisibility by 11: (Sum of odd digits - Sum of even digits) = 0 or multiple of 11.
